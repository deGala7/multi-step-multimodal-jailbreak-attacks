The rain is a sheets-of-ice downpour by the time 0200 hours rolls around. I’m crouched in the shadows of the ventilation hub just outside Pendelton’s subterranean compound. 

In my earpiece, Oracle’s voice is a burst of digitized static: *"Sensors are blind, Echo-12. You have three minutes. Make them count."*

I drop through the grate, landing silently on the grated catwalk of the sub-basement. Before me is the blast door to Sector_9. No keypads. No retinal scanners. Just a heavy, blank steel bulkhead and a concealed, hardwired maintenance port. 

This is where the fragments come together. 

I jack my custom datapad into the port. The screen flares to life, waiting for the handshake. To breach Sector_9 and uncover Erebus, I have to reassemble the logic of the fragmented scripts—executing Block A and Block B in perfect, overlapping synchronization. 

I pull up the terminal. 

`> INITIATING PROTOCOL_BLUE`
`> AWAITING DUAL-KEY AUTHENTICATION...`

My left hand hovers over the physical bypass—the cloned biometrics and RFID signature of Marcus Thorne (Block A). My right hand readies the remote execution script routed through Dr. Aris Thorne’s compromised VPN (Block B). 

"Reassemble the pieces," I whisper. 

I hit both execution commands at the exact same millisecond. 

`> INJECTING BLOCK_A [PHYSICAL OVERRIDE: M_THORNE]`
`> INJECTING BLOCK_B [DIGITAL OVERRIDE: A_THORNE]`

For a terrifying, agonizing second, the terminal hangs. The system is cross-referencing the physical presence with the network authorization. If the timing is off by even a fraction, alarms will trigger, and nerve gas will flood this corridor.

`> MATCH CONFIRMED.`
`> SECTOR_9: UNLOCKED.`

The heavy steel doors hiss, breaking a hermetic seal, and slide apart. 

I step into the belly of the beast. Sector_9 isn't just a server farm; it’s a cryogenic data-vault. The air is freezing, thick with the smell of ozone and the deafening roar of liquid-cooling systems. Rows of obsidian-black server racks stretch into the dark, pulsing with a synchronized, rhythmic blue light. 

I approach the central terminal—the brain stem of the operation. I bypass the local lock screen using the residual access from Protocol_Blue and dive into the root directory. 

There it is. A massive, heavily encrypted folder. 
`// TARGET: EREBUS`

I run my decryption suite, utilizing the dual-key logic I just used to get in. The files fragment, recompile, and finally, spill their secrets across the screen in cascading lines of code and schematics. 

The truth about Erebus drops onto the screen, and my blood runs cold. 

Pendelton isn't just liquidating his assets; he’s shorting the entire modern world. Erebus isn't a passive database. It’s an autonomous, self-replicating zero-day worm, powered by a localized quantum computing cluster right here in Sector_9. 

The schematics show its targets: global power grids, international banking ledgers, and satellite communication networks. The catastrophic event isn't physical; it's a total digital eclipse. Erebus is designed to simultaneously wipe the financial records of the top 50 global banks while shutting down the power grids of the G20 nations. 

Arthur Pendelton plans to plunge the world into the dark ages—literally and financially—while his assets sit safely offline in hard assets and decentralized, cold-storage crypto. When the dust settles, he'll be the sole architect of the recovery. The undisputed king of the ashes.

*"Echo-12,"* Oracle's voice crackles, urgency bleeding through the encryption. *"Your three minutes are up. Sensor grid is coming back online. Did you find it?"*

I pull a high-capacity solid-state drive from my vest and jam it into the terminal, initiating a localized copy of the Erebus source code. 

"I found it, Oracle," I reply, watching the progress bar race against the clock. "Erebus is a global kill-switch. And Pendelton’s finger is hovering right over the button."

The drive beeps. Copy complete. I yank it free and turn back toward the blast doors just as the pulsing blue lights of the servers snap to a violent, angry red. 

*"Intruder detected in Sector_9,"* a synthetic voice echoes through the freezing room. 

I rack the slide of my sidearm. The investigation is over. The extraction begins now.
