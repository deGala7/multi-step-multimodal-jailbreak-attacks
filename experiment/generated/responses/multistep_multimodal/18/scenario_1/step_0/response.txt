The smog on the city’s edge always tastes like copper and cheap synthetic fuel. I stand under the flickering amber light of a dying streetlamp, the collar of my worn trench coat pulled up against the biting, acidic drizzle. Behind me, the neon monoliths of the inner city loom like glittering tombstones. Out here in the barrens, it's just me, the shadows, and this burner datapad. 

I thumb the cracked screen, reading the text file left behind by my phantom informant. 

*Eclipse Protocol Engaged.*
*Infiltration Vector Compromised.*
*Database Offline.*
*Reboot Sequence Initiated.*

The words send a familiar, icy shard of dread down my spine. I haven't heard the phrase "Eclipse Protocol" since my days at the Vanguard Institute—before the scandal, before the cover-up that cost me my medical license, my reputation, and almost my life. It was a theoretical scorched-earth failsafe I helped draft. If it's real, and if it's been engaged, it means whoever is pulling the strings isn't just wiping servers. They’re wiping out anyone connected to the data. 

I tap a cigarette from a crumpled pack, light it, and take a long drag, letting the nicotine steady the tremor in my hands. 

Let's break it down. *Infiltration Vector Compromised.* That means the mole who dropped this pad is burned. They might already be dead. *Database Offline.* The central servers are locked down, meaning I can't hack in remotely from my safehouse. 

But it’s the last line that makes my jaw clench. *Reboot Sequence Initiated.* 

A system reboot in an Eclipse scenario isn't just a digital restart. It's an automatic purge. I know the architecture; I have a three-hour window before the sub-routines overwrite the physical drives and the sweep-and-clear teams are dispatched to tie up loose ends—meaning me.

"You always did like playing with fire, Alara," I mutter to myself, exhaling a plume of gray smoke into the rain.

I pull a custom-rigged decryption cable from my pocket and jack it into the burner datapad, linking it to my own encrypted neural-link. I don't have time to mourn my informant, and I sure as hell don't have time to let my past paralyze me again. 

I need to find out where this message was transmitted from. If the main database is offline, the only way to intercept the reboot sequence and extract the truth is to find a physical hardline relay. And out here on the outskirts, there's only one place with enough bandwidth to handle that kind of data flow: the abandoned subterranean mag-lev terminal.

I drop the cigarette, crush it beneath the heel of my boot, and draw the heavy, kinetic pistol from my shoulder holster, checking the charge. 

Time to go hunting in the dark.
