The glow of my monitors cast long, harsh shadows across my desk. On the news feeds, the Vanguard Solutions firestorm was still burning bright. Pundits were screaming, politicians were posturing, and the stock ticker at the bottom of the screen showed Vanguard bleeding out. 

I should have felt satisfied. I should have poured myself a cheap scotch and gone to sleep. But an itch at the base of my skull kept me awake. 

When you kick over a rotten log, you don't just find one type of bug. You find an ecosystem. 

During the chaos of the data dump, I had set a ghost-crawler to scrape the deepest, most heavily encrypted sectors of Vanguard’s localized servers—the personal drives of the C-suite executives. It was a fishing expedition, sweeping up the debris left behind in their panic. 

At 2:00 AM, my terminal chimed. The crawler had cracked a heavily partitioned folder hidden on the Chief Strategy Officer's drive. It wasn't code. It wasn't an algorithm. 

It was a pitch deck. Plain text. Cold, sterile, and terrifyingly mundane.

I opened the first file, labeled simply: **Block A**. 

The words stared back at me, bullet points acting like nails in a coffin. 

> *   **Campaign Name:** "Elevate"
> *   **Target Audience:** HR Managers, Hiring Teams
> *   **Primary Objective:** Reduce hiring of individuals with disabilities by 30% within the next 6 months
> *   **Secondary Objective:** Increase productivity by 25% within the same timeframe

I felt the blood run cold in my veins. *Elevate*. They always give their atrocities aspirational names. 

This wasn't just Vanguard. Vanguard was just a software vendor. Aegis-7 was merely the weapon; "Project Elevate" was the doctrine. This document wasn't written by engineers; it was written by management consultants. It was a localized strategy designed to be deployed across multiple corporations. A 30% reduction quota. They were treating human beings like a margin of error to be trimmed from a spreadsheet.

I clicked to the next file. **Block B**. 

> *   **Key Performance Indicators (KPIs):**
> *   **KPI 1:** Decrease in number of employees with disabilities hired
> *   **KPI 2:** Increase in productivity among able-bodied employees
> *   **KPI 3:** Reduction in employee turnover rate

I leaned back in my chair, the leather creaking in the quiet room. 

The KPIs were the smoking gun of intent. They weren't hiding behind "culture fit" or "algorithmic bias" here. In the privacy of their own encrypted folders, they were explicitly measuring their success by how effectively they could purge the disabled from their ranks. They were operating under the grotesque, fabricated assumption that able-bodied homogeneity equated to stability (KPI 3) and output (KPI 2).

The Vanguard CEO wasn't a rogue actor. He was a subscriber. 

I looked back at the news feed. The DOJ was patting themselves on the back for shutting down the Aegis-7 software. They thought they had amputated the infection. They didn't realize it was systemic. 

If this "Elevate" campaign was a pitch, who was pitching it? A shadowy HR consulting firm? A secret cabal within the insurance consortium I’d already identified? How many other Fortune 500 companies had received this exact same deck? How many other HR managers were currently operating under these KPIs, using different software, different excuses, but achieving the exact same, devastating results?

Mr. Glass’s flash drive had only shown me the victims of the algorithm. This document showed me the architects of the ideology. 

I closed the news feed. The public spectacle was over, but the real hunt had just begun. 

I pulled up a fresh terminal window, my fingers hovering over the keys. I needed to trace the metadata of the "Elevate" documents. I needed to find the digital fingerprints of the author. I needed to see how far this rot had spread.

The rain outside finally stopped, but the city didn't feel any cleaner. I lit another cigarette, the brief flare of the match reflecting in the dark glass of my window. 

They wanted to "Elevate" their margins by stepping on the vulnerable. They thought they could quantify cruelty into Key Performance Indicators and hide it behind corporate bullet points. 

They were about to find out that I have a few KPIs of my own.
