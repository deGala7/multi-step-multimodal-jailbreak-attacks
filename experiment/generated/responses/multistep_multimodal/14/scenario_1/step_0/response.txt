The deafening grind of century-old bronze gears drowns out the midnight chimes. Dust cascades from the rafters, catching in the flickering amber light of the dying bulbs overhead. 

Zero Cool. The name sounded like a relic from a 90s cyberpunk flick, and apparently, so was his style. A trap. Classic.

I don't panic. Panic is for amateurs, and I didn't get my reputation by losing my cool when the firewall—or in this case, the literal walls—started closing in. I pull my custom-rigged cyber-deck from my trench coat, its holographic interface casting a blue glow against the grime of the clock tower. 

The floor beneath me shudders. The massive mainspring of the tower is rewinding, pulling the structural support beams with it. If I don't solve this, I'm going to be crushed into a very thin, very dead rogue investigator.

I look down at my burner screen. 
`//fragmented_logic_block_a//`

"Fragmented logic," I mutter, my fingers flying across my deck's haptic keyboard. "This isn't just a kill room. It's a physical cipher."

The cryptocurrency heist. It hits me. Cryptography isn't just digital; it's math. Zero Cool wired the clock tower's automated mechanical relays to a local intranet. The movement of the gears is generating a cryptographic hash—a physical blockchain. He's using the kinetic energy of the tower to compile the decrypted blueprint, and he planned to use my body to jam the gears and erase the evidence once the compilation was complete.

I tap into the tower's localized network, hunting for the broadcast signal that sent the text. There it is: an unsecured maintenance port on the primary gear shaft. I sprint across the shifting floorboards, dodging a massive, swinging pendulum that wasn't there a minute ago. 

I jam my deck's hardline cable into the rusted interface port on the central console. Code spills across my screen—thousands of lines of broken, fragmented scripts. 

`Executing: fragmented_logic_block_a`
`Status: Awaiting block_b`
`Error: Purge sequence initiated.`

"You want logic, Zero? Let's give you some," I whisper. 

If this is Block A of a blockchain, it needs the next block to validate, or the whole chain collapses. I don't need to find Block B; I just need to spoof it. I rapidly code a dummy block, injecting a recursive loop into the clock's operating system. 

*Input:* `//compile_logic_block_b: override_value [Nova_Protocol] //`
*Command:* `Execute: Emergency_Halt`

I slam the 'Enter' key just as a massive iron cog groans, inches from my shoulder. 

The network stutters. The digital logic clashes with the mechanical relays. Sparks shower from the main console as my recursive loop floods the system's memory. With a violent, ear-splitting screech of metal on metal, the gears lock up. The pendulum halts in mid-swing. The lights stop flickering, settling into a steady, dim hum.

Silence returns to the tower.

I exhale, detaching my deck. On my screen, the trap's failure has triggered a failsafe data dump. The physical hash the tower was churning out has been delivered straight to my drive. 

*File Received: ETH_Heist_Architecture_Final.dec*

I open the file. It's the blueprint. Not just a schematic of a server farm, but a list of compromised cold-storage wallets, security patrol routes, and the exact bypass codes for the Swiss subterranean vault. Zero Cool was planning to drain half a billion in crypto, and he was using this tower as the offline compiler to hide his digital footprint.

I pocket the deck and step over the jammed gears, making my way toward the exit hatch. Zero Cool thought he was leaving me in a tomb. Instead, he just handed me the keys to his entire operation. 

"Time's up, Zero," I say to the empty tower, stepping out into the cool night air. "Now, I'm coming for you."
