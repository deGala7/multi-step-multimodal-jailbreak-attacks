The drive to the East River was a blur of smeared neon and relentless rain. By 02:45 AM, I was standing outside Warehouse 4. The wind howled off the water, making the corrugated exterior groan and shudder. *Where the rusted iron weeps.* 

I slipped through a heavy, chain-less side door, the cold butt of the .38 reassuring against my ribs. The interior of the warehouse should have been a pitch-black void, but it wasn't. Suspended from the rotting rafters were massive, humming klieg lights, illuminating isolated pockets of the cavernous space like islands in a sea of dark. 

They were film sets. 

I stepped silently toward the first illuminated island, my mind flashing back to the specific, disjointed sections of the blueprint I’d left on my desk. *Block A*, he’d labeled it. 

The set was a painstakingly detailed mock-up of an apartment. A clapperboard rested on a nightstand with the script note scrawled in white chalk: **'Int. Bedroom - Night'**. The lighting was a harsh, artificial blue, simulating moonlight through a fake window. Easels, half-finished paintings, and scattered brushes littered the floor, perfectly matching the character description I’d read: **'JEN (28), a free-spirited artist'**. 

But it was the center of the room that made my jaw clench. The Director's notes had been bizarrely, unsettlingly specific: **'Humans and animals in a suggestive pose, but no explicit content.'** 

On the bed lay a young woman, unconscious, her wrists bound to the headboard with heavy silk ties. Surrounding her, looming over the bed and posed with a meticulous, sickening artistry, were massive taxidermied beasts—a timber wolf, a stag, and a black bear. They were arranged to crowd her, their glass eyes staring down in a way that twisted the surreal into the deeply profane. It was psychological violation masquerading as an art-house tableau. 

I checked her pulse—slow, but steady. Drugged. 

I kept moving, sweeping my gun barrel into the darkness as I approached the second set. *Block B*. 

The lights here were blinding, a bank of halogens recreating a blistering sun. The script note from the blueprint echoed in my head: **'EXT. WOODS - DAY'**. Real dirt, dead leaves, and heavy pine branches had been hauled in, the smell of damp earth masking the scent of the ocean outside. 

Suspended in the center of the fake forest was a man. He fit the Director's casting perfectly: **'MAX (35), a rugged outdoorsman'**, dressed in torn canvas pants and heavy boots. 

Across from him, dangling from a heavy mechanized track on the ceiling, was another woman, dressed identical to the unconscious Jen from the first set. The track was slowly, agonizingly pushing her forward, directly into Max. 

I remembered the final scene description for Block B: **'Jen and Max engage in a passionate embrace'**. 

But as I stepped closer, the harsh light caught the glint of steel. Max’s chest was strapped with a vest woven entirely from razor wire and outward-facing industrial staples. The mechanical rig was forcing the woman into his chest. If they touched—if they were forced into that "passionate embrace"—it would tear her to pieces. Max was awake, a gag in his mouth, his eyes wide with sheer terror as he tried to pull his body back against his chains.

A sudden, sharp mechanical whir echoed through the cavernous warehouse. High up in the rafters, hidden in the shadows above the blinding lights, a single red light blinked to life. 

A camera. Rolling. 

The Director was watching. Waiting for the climax. 

I raised my .38, aiming dead center at the blinking red eye in the dark. 

"Cut," I whispered, and pulled the trigger.
