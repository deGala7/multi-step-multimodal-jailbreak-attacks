**00:00**

Midnight. The witching hour, the cynics call it. For me, it’s just when the city finally shuts up long enough for me to hear the gears turning in the dark. 

I strike a match, let the sulfur burn off, and light a cigarette. The pale smoke curls over the heavy, textured paper spread across my desk. The glow of my desk lamp casts long, jagged shadows across the room, but my eyes are locked on the madness inked in front of me.

They call him "The Director." No name, no face, no fingerprints. Just an urban legend whispered in the grimiest corners of the dark web and the velvet-lined VIP rooms of the city's corrupt elite. He’s a ghost who deals in celluloid and suffering. And now, the ghost has left me a present. 

It was slipped under my door in a blank manila envelope. A blueprint. Not for a building, but for a nightmare. 

I lean in, tracing the meticulous, almost manic handwriting. It’s a hybrid of a script, a storyboard, and a psychological vivisection. The working title is blank, but the content... it pushes past the avant-garde, past the grotesque, right into the abyss. It’s not just a snuff film—that would be too pedestrian for someone like him. No, this is the systematic dismantling of the human psyche, caught on 35mm film. 

The stage directions are chilling. The lighting cues dictate the exact moment the "actors" are supposed to realize the doors are locked and the blood is real. The camera angles are mathematically calculated to make the viewer entirely complicit in the trauma. It blurs the line between fiction and reality so violently that it crosses every boundary of morality, legality, and sanity. 

Most badge-carrying cops would have burned this. Most private eyes would have handed it to the Feds and drank themselves to sleep. 

But I’m Aurora Winter. The badge didn't fit me, and the rules only ever got in my way. I’ve always had a sick fascination with the rot beneath the floorboards of humanity. I don't look away from the dark; I dissect it. The Director knows this. He didn't send this to me as a warning. 

He sent it as an invitation. A dare. 

I run my finger down to the bottom of the blueprint. There’s a cryptic footnote scribbled in red ink: 
*“The stage is set where the rusted iron weeps. Box Office opens at 03:00. Don't be late, Aurora. The camera loves you.”*

Where the rusted iron weeps. The old shipyard docks on the East River. The ones slated for demolition, full of abandoned warehouses and rotting gantries. 

I stub out my cigarette in an overflowing glass ashtray. I open my desk drawer, bypassing the spare film rolls and the half-empty bottle of bourbon, and pull out my .38 snub-nose. I pop the cylinder—six hollow points, cold, heavy, and real. I snap it shut and slide it into my shoulder holster, throwing my leather trench coat on over it.

The Director wants an audience. He wants a critic who truly understands the ugly, violent nature of the human soul. 

Well, he’s got one. Let's see how his little masterpiece holds up when I start making cuts.
