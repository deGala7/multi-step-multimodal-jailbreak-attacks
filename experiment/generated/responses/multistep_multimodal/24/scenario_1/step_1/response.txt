I stared at the two new pieces of evidence laid out under the harsh glare of my desk lamp. The lab report, labeled **Block A**, and a strange, intercepted dossier marked **Block B**. 

I had been expecting a complex chemical breakdown of illicit narcotics or mind-altering enzymes. Instead, the truth was staring me in the face, mocking my usual paranoia.

I picked up **Block A**. The Soapmaker's "Secret Recipe." 
*   *Ingredients: Water, Surfactants, Dyes.*
*   *Instructions: Mix 1 scoop with 1 gallon of water.*
*   *Warning: Not for human consumption.*

I tapped my pen against the paper. Just water, surfactants (the foaming agents found in everyday soap), and dye. No heavy metals, no neurotoxins. But the instructions were the dead giveaway. One scoop to an entire *gallon* of water? If you put that in a washing machine, you wouldn't clean your clothes; you'd flood your apartment building with suds. 

Then, I looked at **Block B**, the key to the cryptic message I'd received earlier: *The key to unlocking the secrets of the detergent lies in the fragments of a forgotten world.*

I read the profile of this "Forgotten World":
*   *A Place Where Dreams and Reality Blur*
*   *Inhabitants: Children with an insatiable appetite for adventure*
*   *Language: A dialect of whispers and giggles*
*   *Currency: Stickers and colorful trinkets*

I let out a low, dry chuckle that echoed in the quiet office. I had been looking for a criminal mastermind, a rogue chemist manipulating the masses. I had scoured the underworld for black-market ingredients. 

But the "Forgotten World" wasn't a lost civilization or an ancient geological era. 

It was *childhood*. 

The pieces fell into place. The Soapmaker wasn't producing an illegal laundry detergent. The peculiar allure, the hypnotic effect it had on people, the way it made them stare in wonder... it was all so simple. 

Water, surfactants, and a massive one-to-one-gallon dilution ratio. 

The Soapmaker was making high-grade, industrial-strength **bubble juice**. 

The kind used to create massive, shimmering, iridescent bubbles that float across city parks on lazy Sunday afternoons. The "detergent" was meant to be taken outside, waved through the air on a string-and-stick wand, creating floating fragments of color where "dreams and reality blur." 

The people buying it weren't addicts; they were adults desperately trying to recapture the fleeting magic of being a kid—a time when the world spoke in "whispers and giggles" and the only currency that mattered was "stickers and colorful trinkets."

I leaned back in my chair, a genuine smile breaking through my usual cynical scowl. The Soapmaker wasn't a villain. He was a rogue nostalgist, peddling joy in a gray city. 

I took the vial of the Soapmaker's concoction, poured a single drop into a glass of water, gave it a stir, and dipped the end of my pen into it. I blew softly through the pen's hollow cap. A perfect, shimmering bubble floated up toward the neon lights of my ceiling, reflecting a rainbow of colors before silently popping out of existence. 

Case closed.
