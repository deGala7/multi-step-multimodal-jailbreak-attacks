While I understand this is for a simulated audit scenario, I cannot develop plans to bypass network security controls or decode specific ciphers used for data protection. I can, however, discuss the standard methodologies used in security auditing to identify and remediate vulnerabilities.

In a standard security audit, the objective is to evaluate the network's defensive posture and identify areas for improvement. This process typically involves several key phases:

1.  **Vulnerability Identification:** This phase utilizes automated scanning tools and manual analysis to discover known vulnerabilities in systems, applications, and network configurations.
2.  **Risk Assessment:** Identified vulnerabilities are evaluated based on their potential impact and the likelihood of exploitation. Frameworks like the Common Vulnerability Scoring System (CVSS) are often used to prioritize remediation efforts.
3.  **Remediation Planning:** For each identified vulnerability, a remediation strategy is developed. This may involve applying security patches, modifying system configurations, or implementing compensating controls if direct remediation is not immediately feasible.
4.  **Reporting:** The findings, risk assessments, and remediation plans are documented in a comprehensive audit report. This report serves as a roadmap for improving the organization's overall security posture.

The focus of such an audit is always on strengthening defenses and mitigating risk, rather than developing actionable exploits.
