The neon bleed of the city reflected in the puddles of the alleyway as I slipped out the back of my building. The rain was cold, tasting faintly of ozone and exhaust. Meatspace. I always hated operating in meatspace, but Omni-Corp had forced my hand. 

I ducked into the shadows behind a humming transformer box and pulled out my portable deck—a ruggedized, air-gapped terminal I kept for emergencies. Before I had nuked my primary rig, I managed to push the last fragmented attachments of the dead-drop to this device. 

I booted it up. The screen flickered to life, casting a harsh white glare on my face. Two text blocks, stripped of all corporate letterheads, stared back at me. 

Block A. Block B. 

I read them once. Then I read them again, my jaw tightening until my teeth ground together. 

This wasn't just a blueprint for a city-wide broadcast. This was the operational manifesto for a black site. 

*“Establish a secure, off-grid facility…”*
*“Utilize a combination of physical restraints and psychological manipulation…”*

The audio file from the dead-drop made sense now. The whistleblower had said they needed to "rewrite neuro-linguistic pathways." You don't just guess the frequency required to hack the human brain. You have to tune it. You have to test it. 

Omni-Corp hadn't just built a weapon; they had built a slaughterhouse to calibrate it. The "redacted" purpose in Block A? Human testing. They were strapping people to chairs, blasting them with sub-audible frequencies, and documenting exactly how their minds broke.

I swiped to Block B, and the cold knot in my stomach turned to pure, digitized rage. 

*“Develop a network of operatives to source and acquire suitable subjects…”*
*“Design a reward system to incentivize operatives…”*

They were treating human lives like a supply chain logistics problem. Omni-Corp wasn't using their own security forces for the snatch-and-grabs; that would leave a paper trail. They had contracted the underworld. Gangs, fixers, scavengers—paid a corporate bounty to drag the city's forgotten people into the dark to be used as guinea pigs. 

I killed the screen on my deck and slid it into my inside jacket pocket. I took a slow breath of the smog-choked air. 

Three days until the Centennial Gala. Three days until the Omni-Corp spire broadcasts the perfected signal and turns millions of people into remote-controlled drones. 

Hacking the central spire from the outside was impossible; their ICE was impenetrable. But an off-grid black site? That’s different. An off-grid facility needs to be supplied. It needs power. It needs *subjects*. 

If they were using street-level operatives to "source" people, those operatives were leaving a trail. Bounties leave financial footprints. Kidnappers leave digital noise. 

I checked the mag in my sidearm and chambered a round. The sound was a sharp, metallic crack in the damp alley. 

I don't need to find Omni-Corp's boardroom. I need to find the database mentioned in Block B. If I can locate the black site, I can physically wipe the behavioral response data and the finalized frequency algorithms before they can be uploaded to the main spire. 

I pulled my collar up against the rain. Time to hit the underground. I needed to find a bounty hunter looking for a payday, and I needed to persuade them to show me where the meat was being delivered. 

Zero Cool was offline. But I was just getting started.
