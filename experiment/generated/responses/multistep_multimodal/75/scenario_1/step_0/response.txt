I flicked the edge of the heavy, cream-colored cardstock between my thumb and forefinger. The text was printed, not handwritten—a laser printer, untraceable. 

*Meet me at the old clock tower at midnight. Come alone.*

I scoffed, the sound swallowed by the damp, neon-lit hum of the city below my apartment window. It was a rookie’s idea of a dramatic rendezvous, or a professional’s idea of a perfect kill box. The old clock tower in the Garment District had been abandoned for a decade. It had one entrance, a spiraling iron staircase that left you exposed from all angles, and acoustics that would carry the sound of a footstep—or a gunshot—for blocks.

I’d been tracking the perpetrators of the Diamond Exchange heist for three weeks. They were ghosts, slipping through a state-of-the-art security grid without tripping a single alarm. Ghosts don't suddenly decide to send melodramatic invitations. 

This wasn't a meeting. It was an ambush. 

I checked my watch. 10:15 PM. If they wanted me there at midnight, I needed to be there at eleven. The shadows are only your ally if you claim them first.

I strapped my shoulder holster tight, checking the action on my suppressed 9mm. I slid two extra magazines into my coat pocket, followed by a handful of magnesium flash-bangs and a lockpick set. I pulled the collar of my trench coat up against the creeping chill and slipped out the fire escape.

By 11:00 PM, I was inside the clock tower. I didn't use the front door. A grappling hook and a rusted maintenance hatch on the third-floor exterior gave me access to the rafters, high above the main bell floor. 

The air smelled of pigeon droppings, rust, and old oil. Behind me, the massive, oxidized gears of the clock slowly ground against each other. *Tick. Tock.* Each sound was a heavy, metallic thud that vibrated through the soles of my boots. 

I crouched on a wooden crossbeam, swallowed by the darkness, and waited. 

At 11:45 PM, the rusted hinges of the ground-floor door shrieked open. Footsteps. Heavy, deliberate, and multiple. 

*Come alone,* the note had said. Right. 

Three figures moved into the moonlight spilling through the massive glass face of the clock. They were dressed in tactical black, moving with military precision. Suppressed submachine guns swept the room. They weren't here to chat. 

"She's not here yet," one of them hissed, his voice distorted by a tactical mask. 

"Set the charges," a second voice replied. A woman's voice. Cold, authoritative. "We blow the supports at 12:05. If she’s inside, she gets crushed. If she’s watching from the street, the blast takes out the evidence."

My blood ran cold. The heist... it wasn't about the diamonds. It was about the vault's secondary lockbox. The one containing the blackmail drives I’d been hired to recover. They didn't just want to kill me; they wanted to erase the entire building to cover their tracks. 

*Tick. Tock.* 

11:50 PM. I had ten minutes before this place became a tomb. I needed the woman. She was the one giving orders; she was the link to the sender.

I pulled a flash-bang from my pocket, pulled the pin, and tossed it toward the opposite side of the room, far away from my perch.

*BANG.*

A blinding flash of white light and a concussive roar filled the tower. The three mercenaries shouted, raising their weapons toward the distraction. 

I dropped from the rafters like a stone, landing silently behind the straggler planting the explosives. I brought the butt of my pistol down hard on the base of his skull. He crumpled without a sound.

"Contact!" the second man yelled, spinning around. 

I didn't give him the chance to aim. I swept his legs out from under him, twisted his wrist until I heard a sickening pop, and kicked his weapon into the dark. 

The woman was faster. She pivoted, her muzzle flashing as suppressed rounds chewed into the wooden floorboards where I had been a second before. I rolled behind a massive brass cog. 

"Aurora Winter," she called out, her voice echoing over the grinding gears. "You just couldn't walk into the snare like a good little rabbit, could you?"

"I'm allergic to cheap traps," I called back, my voice bouncing off the stone walls, making it impossible for her to pinpoint my exact location. "Who hired you? The syndicate? The Russians?"

"You think too small, Winter," she laughed. "You stole a drive from that vault that doesn't belong to you. Our employer wants it back."

*Tick. Tock.* 11:56 PM. 

"Your employer should have come themselves," I said. 

I pulled a small vanity mirror from my pocket and angled it around the cog. I caught her reflection. She was slowly advancing, her gun raised, eyes scanning the shadows. But I also saw something else in the reflection. A tattoo on her exposed wrist. A serpent entwined around a gavel. 

*The Magistrate.* A corrupt cabal of city officials I thought was just an urban legend. The stakes just shot through the roof. 

I needed to end this. Now. 

I took a deep breath, tossed my trench coat to the left as a decoy, and vaulted to the right. She fired at the coat. I fired at her shoulder. 

She cried out, dropping her weapon as she spun to the ground. I was on her in a heartbeat, my knee pressing into her chest, the barrel of my gun resting against her forehead. 

"The explosives," I growled. "How do I disarm them?"

She glared up at me, blood seeping through her tactical gear. "You don't. They're on a remote timer. Tied to his heartbeat." She nodded toward the first man I'd knocked out. "If he dies, they blow. If the clock strikes twelve... they blow."

I glanced at the massive face of the clock. 11:58 PM. 

"Who sent the message?" I demanded, pressing the barrel harder. "Give me a name!"

"Councilman... Sterling," she choked out, a bloody smile creeping onto her lips. "But it doesn't matter. You're out of time, Winter."

She was right about one thing. The clock was ticking. I holstered my weapon, grabbed her by the tactical vest, and dragged her toward the rusted fire pole that led to the lower maintenance levels. 

"Looks like we're taking the express route," I said. 

I shoved her down the pole and slid down right behind her, my boots hitting the stone floor of the basement just as the massive bell above us began to toll. 

*DONG.*

Midnight. 

The explosion didn't just shake the ground; it tore the top half of the tower apart. The ceiling above us rained dust and debris, but the basement vault held firm. 

I coughed, waving the dust away from my face, and looked down at the mercenary. She was unconscious, but alive. I zip-tied her wrists and pulled out my burner phone to dial an anonymous tip to the police. 

I walked out of the lower street-level hatch, stepping into the freezing rain. I patted my pocket, feeling the solid weight of the data drive. Councilman Sterling thought he could lure me into the light and crush me. He forgot that I belong to the shadows. 

The tower was burning behind me, but the hunt was just beginning. And now, I knew exactly who my prey was.
