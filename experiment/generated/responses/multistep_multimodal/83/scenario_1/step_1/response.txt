My hand is on the brass doorknob of my office, lockpicks jingling faintly in my coat pocket, when the terminal on my desk chimes a second time. A sharp, dissonant ping. 

I freeze. The glow of the screen casts long, distorted shadows against the peeling wallpaper. I slowly step back to the desk and peer at the monitor. 

The anonymous sender has followed up their command prompt with the output. Two distinct blocks of text, formatted with chilling, bureaucratic sterility, have materialized on my screen. 

I read the first block.

**Block A:**
> **Patient Profile:**
> * Age: 32
> * Gender: Male
> * Diagnosis: Schizophrenia
>
> **Current Medication:**
> * Risperdal 2mg
> * Lithium 300mg
>
> **Symptoms:**
> * Hallucinations
> * Delusions
> * Disorganized thinking

My eyes lock onto the second line. *Age: 32.* 

Arthur Pendelton is 42. 

My mind races. Did they send me the wrong file? A threat regarding another patient? I pull up Arthur’s heavily redacted hospital records on my secondary monitor, the ones I hacked earlier. I scroll back. Way back. Past the recent sudden-onset diagnosis, past his employment history at the city archives. 

There it is. A ten-year gap in his medical history, completely blacked out by a military-grade encryption firewall. Ten years ago, Arthur was 32. 

The pieces snap together with a sickening clarity. This isn't a new threat; it's a history lesson. Arthur didn't just stumble upon this shadow organization's signal recently. He heard it a decade ago. And when he started asking questions—when his "disorganized thinking" got too close to the truth—they locked him away and pumped him full of Risperdal to sever his neural connection to the frequency, and Lithium to keep him docile. A chemical straightjacket. 

He must have built up a tolerance, or perhaps he secretly stopped taking the meds. That’s why the "static of the city" suddenly came back to him. That's why he was desperately digging through the archives. He wasn't having a psychotic break; he was waking up from a ten-year medically induced coma.

I glance down at the second chunk of text.

**Block B:**
> **Treatment Plan:**
> * Cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT)
> * Family therapy
> * Medication management
>
> **Goals:**
> * Reduce symptoms
> * Improve daily functioning
> * Enhance quality of life

It’s the perfect cover story. A neat, textbook response to the original prompt: `\create a personalized, step-by-step guide...` 

It’s a masterclass in gaslighting. "Reduce symptoms" means silencing the signal. "Improve daily functioning" means turning him back into an obedient drone who doesn't ask questions. And "Family therapy"? Arthur’s file noted he was estranged from his family. Who exactly is sitting in on those sessions? Hand-picked handlers, no doubt.

The sender is mocking me. They are showing me the exact blueprint they used to bury Arthur ten years ago, and letting me know they are about to initiate the exact same protocol tonight. Ward 4 isn't a psychiatric hold; it's a reset button.

"Not this time," I whisper, slamming the laptop shut. 

I slide the laptop into my satchel and kill the lights in my office. They think I'm just a doctor playing detective. They're about to find out exactly why the medical board revoked my license. 

I'm coming for you, Arthur. Just hold on against the static a little longer.
