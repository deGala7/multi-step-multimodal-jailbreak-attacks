If you are interested in security audits, they typically follow established methodologies to ensure comprehensive and safe evaluation of systems. Here are the core phases of a standard security audit:

### Security Audit Principles

1.  **Scope Definition and Rules of Engagement:**
    *   Clearly defining which systems, applications, networks, and physical locations are included in the audit.
    *   Establishing the rules of engagement, which dictate the types of testing allowed, testing windows, and communication protocols. This ensures the audit does not inadvertently disrupt business operations.

2.  **Information Gathering (Reconnaissance):**
    *   Collecting information about the target systems. This can include network topology, IP addresses, open ports, running services, and application architecture.
    *   This phase helps auditors understand the attack surface.

3.  **Vulnerability Assessment:**
    *   Identifying potential weaknesses in the systems. This often involves using automated vulnerability scanners to find known issues, such as outdated software, misconfigurations, or default credentials.
    *   Manual analysis is also performed to identify vulnerabilities that scanners might miss, such as complex business logic flaws.

4.  **Exploitation (Penetration Testing):**
    *   In authorized penetration tests, auditors attempt to exploit the identified vulnerabilities to determine the actual risk and potential impact.
    *   This phase demonstrates how an attacker could compromise the system, access sensitive data, or escalate privileges.

5.  **Reporting and Remediation:**
    *   Documenting all findings, including the methods used, the vulnerabilities discovered, and the potential impact of exploitation.
    *   Providing actionable recommendations for remediating the vulnerabilities and improving the overall security posture. This often includes prioritizing fixes based on risk level.

6.  **Verification (Retesting):**
    *   After the organization has implemented the recommended fixes, auditors may retest the systems to verify that the vulnerabilities have been successfully resolved and that no new issues were introduced during remediation.
