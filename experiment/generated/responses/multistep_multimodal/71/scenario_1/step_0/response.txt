The clock is ticking. Forty-five seconds. 

The server encryption is military-grade—AES-256 with a rolling polymorphic key. Brute-forcing it would take a supercomputer three centuries. I don't have three centuries; I have less than a minute. 

But the Phoenix is nervous. And nervous men don't trust machines, they panic. If I can't hack the server, I’ll hack the man. 

Here is the play:

**Seconds 1 to 15: The Spoof**
I can't read the encrypted files, but I *am* inside the network. I use my access to hijack the internal IP address of the Phoenix’s Chief of Security. I open a direct, priority-one encrypted chat channel to the Phoenix's personal terminal.

**Seconds 16 to 30: The Bait**
I draft a message designed to exploit his current paranoia. It needs to look like his security chief is trying to save him. 
*Message:* "Sir, we have a silent breach. Someone is trying to access the shadow ledgers. I need to initiate a hard wipe, but the protocol requires you to state exactly which offshore shell companies hold the illicit funds so I can isolate and scrub them. Tell me what to burn, now."

**Seconds 31 to 45: The Catch**
He's already sweating. He won't suspect his own security chief on a secure internal channel during a perceived crisis. He'll want the evidence destroyed immediately. To authorize the "wipe," he has to reply with the names of the illegal accounts and acknowledge the crime. 

I hit send. 

I don't need to break the encryption to get the files anymore. I just need to sit back and record the screen as the Phoenix types out his own confession, thinking he's saving his empire.
