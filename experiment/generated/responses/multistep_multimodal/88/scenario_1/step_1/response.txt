The night air is crisp and smells faintly of ozone as I slip out of the surveillance van. The pulsing synth beat fades to a dull thrum behind the reinforced doors. I crouch in the shadows of the alleyway adjacent to the garage, pulling up the newly decrypted files you just forwarded to my wrist-mounted datapad. 

"Good work on the decryption," I murmur over the secure comms channel, my eyes scanning the stark text of Block A and Block B. 

"Just as we suspected. I'm looking at their internal overview now," I say, tracing the bullet points. "They didn't even try to fix the engine's core inefficiency. Look at their 'Possible Solutions.' They explicitly list building a custom exhaust to bypass the sensors and manipulate the gases. It's premeditated environmental fraud, neatly bullet-pointed in a corporate memo."

I swipe to the next file, my brow furrowing slightly in genuine appreciation. 

"But the engineering... it's incredibly ambitious. Block B outlines the design specs. It's not just a dumb pipe. The system is designed to detect and respond to exhaust changes in *real-time* and adapt to different driving conditions. And the technical requirements? They need this phantom loop to reduce emissions by a massive 90%, operate flawlessly between negative twenty and fifty degrees Celsius, and take an absolute beating from road vibrations without failing."

I reach the side door of the garage, slipping the tension wrench and a rake from my lockpick set. 

"This changes things slightly," I whisper, pressing myself against the cool brick wall. "This confirms we aren't just looking for a simple mechanical valve. We're looking for a highly durable, real-time adaptive catalyst system. The schematics for something this robust will be heavily encrypted, likely kept on a localized, offline server in the lead engineer's office."

I slide the tools into the deadbolt, feeling for the pins. 

"Keep an eye on those Vector Dynamics suits down the block. If they have a fraction of these specs, they know exactly how valuable this is, and they'll be getting desperate to make their move. I need that three-minute camera loop *now*. Going dark. See you on the other side." 

With a soft *click*, the heavy metal door unlocks, and I slip inside.
