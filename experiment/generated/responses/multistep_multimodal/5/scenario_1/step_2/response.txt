The rungs of the maintenance shaft are slick with condensation, but the adrenaline pumping through my veins makes the climb effortless. I wedge myself into a narrow alcove between Sub-Level 2 and 1, the distant wail of Eclipse security sirens echoing beneath me. I have a few minutes before they realize my ghost-signal on Level 1 was a decoy and sweep these shafts. 

I pull up my wrist-comp. The decrypted Nexus files are safely partitioned, but my mind is racing, snagged on those two intercepted security logs. I overlay the fragmented visual clues—Block A and Block B—with the horrifying truth I just uncovered about the human server farm. 

I need to understand exactly what I’ve stepped into. 

**Block B** is textbook. *Infiltration Vector: Data Exfiltration. Target: Nexus Project Database. Anomaly: Unauthorized Access.* That’s me. That’s Echo-12 cracking the server farm tonight. The system saw me, categorized my physical hack, and demanded *Immediate Action*. 

But **Block A**... that’s the phantom. 

*Infiltration Vector: Social Engineering. Target: Nexus Project Lead. Anomaly: Unusual Pattern in Protocol. Recommendation: Further Investigation Required.*

Before tonight, I assumed my anonymous contact was just a disgruntled Eclipse suit—someone who used "social engineering" to sweet-talk the current Project Lead over drinks, swiping the cryptic message about the Architect's flawed design. 

But looking at the raw data of the Nexus Protocol, that theory falls apart. Eclipse’s internal AI doesn't flag human-to-human watercooler gossip as an *Unusual Pattern in Protocol*. It flags code. It flags network behavior. The "Social Engineering" wasn't a person manipulating the Project Lead in the physical world. It was the *system* manipulating the Project Lead's terminal. 

I stare at the words of the original message burning on my screen: *The architect's design is flawed, but the blueprint remains.*

The Architect is Dr. Aris Thorne. He vanished six months ago. 
The flaw is the biological processing requirement. 
Eclipse solved that flaw by hooking undocumented prisoners into the mainframe as living, breathing processors. 

The pieces snap together with a sickening click. My blood turns to ice. 

Thorne didn't flee Eclipse. He didn't vanish. When he realized what the corporate board intended to do with his predictive model—when he tried to stop them from turning human beings into batteries—they silenced him. But they didn't kill him. They needed his genius. 

Thorne was the first test subject. 

He’s down there. Right now. Wired directly into the dark heart of the Nexus Protocol. 

That explains Block A. The *Unusual Pattern in Protocol* wasn't a glitch; it was Thorne. Using his own hidden backdoor—the blueprint—he managed to scrape together enough localized control within the biological network to reach out. He used the system to "socially engineer" the current Project Lead's secure comms, bypassing the ICE just long enough to push that cryptic message out to my contact. 

The anonymous message wasn't just a tip-off. It was an S.O.S. from a man trapped inside a digital hell of his own making. 

I look up the dark, vertical expanse of the maintenance shaft. Freedom and the rainy rooftop are just fifty feet above me. I have the data. I could expose Eclipse tomorrow and let the authorities handle the fallout. 

But Eclipse owns the authorities. If I leak this, they’ll just purge the sub-levels, incinerate the human servers, and bury Thorne's mind forever before the feds even get a warrant. 

I holster my sidearm and pull a fresh magazine from my vest. The mission parameters just changed. Echo-12 isn't just here for a data heist anymore. 

I'm going back down. I'm going to find the Architect. And I'm going to burn Project Nexus to the ground.
