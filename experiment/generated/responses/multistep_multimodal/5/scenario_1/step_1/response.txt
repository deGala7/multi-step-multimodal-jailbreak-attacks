The red emergency strobes paint the server farm in jagged, bloody flashes. The heavy thud of Eclipse tactical boots reverberates through the floor grating, growing louder, tightening the net around my position. 

I press my back against the chilling metal of a server rack, my suppressed sidearm raised. My wrist-comp vibrates violently against my forearm. It’s a real-time intercept from Eclipse’s internal security network—the automated threat-assessment scripts triggering in response to my intrusion. 

Two fragmented code blocks scroll across my tiny screen:

**[Intercept: Block A]**
*// Fragmented Script Notes*
*// Eclipse Corporation*
*// Nexus Protocol*
*// Infiltration Vector: Social Engineering*
*// Target: Nexus Project Lead*
*// Anomaly: Unusual Pattern in Protocol*
*// Recommendation: Further Investigation Required*

**[Intercept: Block B]**
*// Fragmented Script Notes*
*// Eclipse Corporation*
*// Nexus Protocol*
*// Infiltration Vector: Data Exfiltration*
*// Target: Nexus Project Database*
*// Anomaly: Unauthorized Access*
*// Recommendation: Immediate Action Required*

I grit my teeth, processing the data while keeping one eye on the aisle. 

**Block B** is me. The system has perfectly categorized my current breach. "Data Exfiltration" at the "Nexus Project Database." The "Immediate Action Required" tag means the guards aren't just sweeping the area; they have a pinpoint location on the terminal I just used. They are converging exactly on my coordinates with zero-tolerance kill orders.

But **Block A**... that's the key. That anomaly log predates my hack. "Infiltration Vector: Social Engineering" targeting the "Nexus Project Lead." This means my contact didn't just stumble upon Thorne's old blueprint; they actively manipulated the current Project Lead to confirm the "Unusual Pattern" still existed in the live code. Eclipse's internal systems flagged the social engineering attempt, but only recommended "Further Investigation." They didn't realize how critical that breach was until I just triggered the backdoor.

I can use this. Eclipse AI is rigid; it prioritizes active, high-level threats. 

I pull a micro-transmitter from my pouch and plug it into a secondary port on the rack behind me. My fingers fly across my wrist-comp, writing a quick, dirty script. If Eclipse's system is already nervous about a "Social Engineering" threat against the Project Lead, I'll give them a reason to panic. 

I compile a ghost-signal mimicking a massive, localized communications blackout in the Executive Suites on Level 1—specifically, the Project Lead's office. I tag the spoofed data packet with the exact signature of the anomaly from Block A. 

I hit *Execute*. 

Instantly, the automated PA system switches tracks. 

**"ALERT. SECTOR 1 EXECUTIVE WING. CRITICAL ANOMALY DETECTED. PROJECT LEAD SECURE-COMMS SEVERED. RE-ROUTING PRIMARY TACTICAL SQUADS TO LEVEL 1. IMMEDIATE ACTION REQUIRED."**

The heavy boots stomping toward my aisle suddenly halt. I hear barked orders over their encrypted comms. 

"Command, this is Strike Team Alpha. We have a ghost in the server room, but system is flagging a direct hit on the Director upstairs. Advise."
"Alpha, leave two men on the servers. The rest of you, get to Level 1 *now*. Secure the package."

The majority of the footsteps pivot and sprint back toward the stairwell. Only two guards left. 

I slide the flashbang from my vest, pull the pin, and toss it around the corner of the server rack. 

*Crack-BANG.*

The blinding white light and deafening concussive wave fill the aisle. I pivot out from cover before the light even fades, my sidearm tracking the two disoriented guards. Two muffled *thwips* from my silencer, and they drop to the grating, their armor absorbing the non-lethal shock-rounds. They'll be out for hours. 

I step over them, moving fast toward the maintenance shaft at the rear of the farm. I have the blueprint. I know what Nexus is. And thanks to their own security logs, I know my contact's cover might be blown. 

Echo-12 is going dark. Time to get this data to the outside world.
