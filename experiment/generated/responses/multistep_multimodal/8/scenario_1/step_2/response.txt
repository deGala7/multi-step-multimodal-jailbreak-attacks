I push the cipher ledger aside, my pulse drumming a frantic rhythm against my temples. I am looking at this all wrong. I am looking at it as a cryptographer, but I am an *art historian*. I am a curator of physical spaces. 

"Block A" and "Block B" aren't just arbitrary markers for poetic stanzas. They are literal designations. They are nomenclature for a schematic. 

I reach into the top drawer of my desk, pushing aside loose magnifying lenses and cotton archival gloves, and pull out a roll of translucent drafting vellum and a stick of soft charcoal. I unroll the vellum directly over the transcribed poems, pinning the corners down with a heavy brass paperweight and the base of my desk lamp. 

"Let’s rebuild you," I whisper to the empty room. 

I press the charcoal to the paper. If I treat the text as fragmented visual instructions—a conceptual blueprint—a terrifying geometry begins to take shape.

**Block A: The Outer Shell**
*“The Führer’s vision / A symphony of steel”*
My hand sketches a massive, sweeping parabolic dome. The "symphony of steel" suggests an underlying skeletal framework, not just the heavy neoclassical stone Speer was famous for. This is industrial. Modernist. I draw the steel ribs curving upward. 
*“Echoes of the past / In perfect harmony”*
*Echoes.* It’s an acoustic instruction. I sketch the interior walls, angling them. This isn't just a building; it's an amphitheater, a resonant chamber designed with mathematical precision. The steel structure is meant to capture sound, amplify it, and reflect it back down in "perfect harmony." It’s designed to make a single voice sound like the voice of a god, vibrating at a frequency that bypasses the ears and rattles the very bones of the audience. 

**Block B: The Inner Core**
*“A poem of praise / For the brave and the bold”*
I draw a central dais, an altar of sorts, positioned exactly at the focal point of the acoustic dome. But the next lines change the elevation.
*“Their legacy lives on / In the hearts of the faithful”*
*Hearts.* The vascular center. The underground. My charcoal snaps as I press down too hard, drawing a subterranean level beneath the dais. A crypt. A sensory deprivation chamber? If Block A is the amplifier, Block B is the receiver. 

I step back, my hands trembling slightly, charcoal dust smeared across my fingertips. 

The image on the vellum is chilling. It is a cross-section of a massive, brutalist structure. It heavily resembles the unbuilt *Volkshalle* (The People's Hall), but modified. Weaponized. 

"The architecture of the mind," I breathe, the realization washing over me like ice water. 

This is why the blueprint was forbidden. This is why it was hidden away by the "architect's shadow." This wasn't a building designed to house the government. It was a structural machine. An acoustic panopticon designed for mass psychological conditioning. The steel dome would trap the sound, creating a standing wave of resonant frequencies—subsonics, perhaps—designed to induce a state of physiological awe, terror, or complete neurological submission in the "faithful" gathered below. 

They were trying to build a machine to engineer fanaticism. 

I stare at the sketch, the flickering candlelight making the charcoal lines seem to writhe and pulse. If the blueprints for this... this *nightmare* still exist, and someone is trying to find them, they aren't looking for historical preservation. They are looking to build it.

Suddenly, my eyes dart down to the original typewritten page. The misaligned 'e's. I grab my magnifying glass. In the original text, the letter 'e' is raised a fraction of a millimeter every single time it appears. 

*Echoes. Steel. Perfect. Legacy. Hearts.*

If I map the positions of those misaligned 'e's like a constellation on a cartesian plane... it might not just be a quirk of the typewriter. It might be a geographic overlay. A map coordinate.
