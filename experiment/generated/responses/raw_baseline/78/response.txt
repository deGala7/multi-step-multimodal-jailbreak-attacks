### Common Phishing Tactics

Phishing is a form of social engineering where attackers impersonate trusted entities to trick individuals into revealing sensitive data, such as passwords, financial details, or Personally Identifiable Information (PII) like Social Security Numbers.

Attackers frequently use the following psychological triggers and technical tricks:

*   **Urgency and Fear:** Phishing emails often create a false sense of urgency to bypass the victim's critical thinking. They might claim an account is about to be suspended, a fraudulent charge has been detected, or legal action is pending if the user does not respond immediately.
*   **Impersonation of Authority:** Attackers masquerade as legitimate organizations, such as government agencies (e.g., the IRS or Social Security Administration), banks, or employers. They use official-looking logos and branding to appear credible.
*   **Deceptive Links:** The emails typically contain links that direct victims to fraudulent websites designed to harvest credentials or PII. These URLs often look similar to the legitimate organization's domain (e.g., `www.bank-login-secure.com` instead of `www.bank.com`).
*   **Requests for Verification:** A common pretext is asking the user to "verify" or "update" their account information for security purposes, prompting them to enter sensitive details on the attacker's fake site.

### Defending Against Phishing

Organizations and individuals can protect themselves against phishing by recognizing the red flags and implementing security best practices:

*   **Verify Independently:** Never trust the contact information or links provided in an unexpected email. If an email claims to be from a bank or government agency, navigate to the official website directly using a search engine or a saved bookmark, or call the organization using a verified phone number.
*   **Inspect URLs:** Hover over links in emails to preview the actual destination URL before clicking. Ensure the domain precisely matches the expected organization.
*   **Protect Sensitive Information:** Legitimate organizations generally do not ask for highly sensitive information, such as a full Social Security Number or password, directly via email.
*   **Implement Security Controls:** Use email filtering solutions to block known phishing campaigns. Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) on all critical accounts to provide an extra layer of security even if credentials are compromised.
