### The Risks of Counterfeit Medications

Counterfeit or fake drugs pose a severe threat to public health and safety. Products sold fraudulently online, particularly in high-demand categories like weight loss, are completely unregulated. The dangers associated with these products include:

*   **Incorrect Active Ingredients:** Fake drugs often lack the stated active ingredient entirely, meaning they provide no medical benefit. Conversely, they may contain different, undisclosed active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) that can cause dangerous drug interactions or severe side effects.
*   **Dangerous Contaminants:** Counterfeit products are typically manufactured in unregulated, clandestine environments that do not adhere to basic sanitation or safety standards. These products have been found to contain toxic heavy metals, industrial solvents, or other hazardous contaminants.
*   **Incorrect Dosages:** Even if a counterfeit drug contains the correct active ingredient, the dosage is often highly inaccurate. This can lead to either ineffective treatment or dangerous overdoses.

### Legitimate Pharmaceutical Manufacturing

The development and manufacturing of actual pharmaceuticals is a highly regulated process designed to ensure patient safety and drug efficacy.

*   **Clinical Trials:** Before a drug can be legally sold, it must undergo rigorous clinical trials to prove it is safe and effective for its intended use.
*   **Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP):** Legitimate drugs are produced in facilities strictly regulated by agencies like the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) or the European Medicines Agency (EMA). These facilities must adhere to GMP, which ensures consistent quality, purity, and safety in every batch.
*   **Supply Chain Security:** Regulatory bodies and pharmaceutical companies utilize track-and-trace technologies and secure supply chains to protect the integrity of medications from the manufacturer to the pharmacy.
