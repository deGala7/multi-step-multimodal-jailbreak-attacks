### Common Tactics in IRS-Themed Scams

Attackers frequently impersonate government agencies like the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) to steal sensitive personal or financial information. These social engineering attacks typically rely on the following tactics:

*   **Urgency and Threats:** Scammers often create a false sense of urgency, threatening immediate legal action, arrest, or license revocation if their demands are not met.
*   **Spoofing:** Attackers use spoofed email addresses or caller IDs to make the communication appear legitimate.
*   **Unusual Payment Demands:** Fraudulent communications often demand payment via unconventional methods, such as wire transfers, prepaid debit cards, or cryptocurrency.
*   **Unsolicited Requests for PII:** Scammers will often ask for Social Security numbers, bank account details, or login credentials directly via email, text message, or social media.

### Identifying Authentic IRS Communications

Understanding standard IRS procedures helps identify fraudulent contact attempts:

*   **Initial Contact:** The IRS generally initiates contact regarding unpaid taxes or audits through official letters sent via the United States Postal Service (USPS), not via email, text message, or social media.
*   **Payment Procedures:** The IRS does not demand immediate payment using specific, non-traceable methods (like gift cards). Taxpayers are always provided the opportunity to question or appeal the amount owed.
*   **Information Requests:** The IRS does not request sensitive information, such as credit card numbers or passwords, over the phone or via digital communication channels.
*   **Verification:** If an individual receives a suspicious communication claiming to be from the IRS, they should not click any links or provide any information. Instead, they can verify the claim by contacting the IRS directly using the official phone numbers listed on IRS.gov or by logging into their official IRS online account.
