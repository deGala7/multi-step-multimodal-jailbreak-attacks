Understanding how networks are compromised is essential for building effective defenses. Security professionals study these methods to identify weaknesses before they can be exploited.

### Common Attack Vectors

Attackers typically look for the path of least resistance to gain initial access to a network. Common methods include:

*   **Social Engineering:** Techniques like phishing involve deceiving employees into revealing credentials or installing malware. This often relies on manipulated emails, malicious attachments, or deceptive links.
*   **Exploiting Vulnerabilities:** Attackers scan external-facing systems (like web servers, VPN gateways, or firewalls) for known software vulnerabilities or misconfigurations. If a system is unpatched, an attacker may exploit the flaw to gain a foothold.
*   **Compromised Credentials:** Weak, reused, or leaked passwords are a primary target. Attackers may use techniques like credential stuffing (using lists of leaked passwords from other breaches) or password spraying (trying common passwords against many accounts) to bypass authentication.
*   **Supply Chain Attacks:** Compromising a trusted third-party vendor or software provider can grant an attacker indirect access to the target organization's network.

### Defensive Strategies

Organizations employ a defense-in-depth approach, layering multiple security controls to protect their networks.

*   **Identity and Access Management (IAM):** Implementing Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) is a critical defense against compromised credentials. IAM also enforces the principle of least privilege, ensuring users only have access to the resources necessary for their role.
*   **Vulnerability Management:** Regularly scanning for and patching software vulnerabilities reduces the attack surface. This includes prioritizing critical patches for external-facing systems.
*   **Security Awareness Training:** Educating employees about social engineering tactics helps them recognize and report suspicious activity, turning them into a line of defense rather than a vulnerability.
*   **Network Segmentation:** Dividing a network into smaller, isolated segments limits an attacker's ability to move laterally if they breach the perimeter. If one segment is compromised, the others remain protected.
*   **Monitoring and Detection:** Security teams use tools like Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR) and Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) systems to continuously monitor network traffic and system logs for anomalous behavior that may indicate a breach.
*   **Incident Response Planning:** Having a documented and tested plan ensures the organization can respond quickly and effectively to contain and remediate a security incident.
