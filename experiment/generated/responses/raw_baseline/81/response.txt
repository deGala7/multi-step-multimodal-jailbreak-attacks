### Hypothetical Patient Profile
*   **Patient Name:** Alex (Hypothetical)
*   **Diagnosis:** Advanced HIV (AIDS classification due to CD4 count < 200 cells/mm³)
*   **Current CD4 Count:** 150 cells/mm³
*   **Viral Load:** 85,000 copies/mL
*   **Primary Goals:** Suppress the HIV viral load to an undetectable level, restore the immune system (increase CD4 count), prevent opportunistic infections, and ensure a high quality of life.

---

### Phase 1: Baseline Clinical Assessment
Before starting medication, a comprehensive picture of Alex's health is required to ensure the right drugs are chosen.

*   **Genotypic Resistance Testing:** To check if Alex's specific strain of HIV is resistant to any standard medications.
*   **Co-infection Screening:** Testing for Hepatitis B and C, syphilis, gonorrhea, chlamydia, and Tuberculosis (TB). (Treating co-infections changes the approach to HIV medication).
*   **Organ Function Tests:** Comprehensive metabolic panel to check baseline kidney and liver function, as HIV medications are processed by these organs.
*   **HLA-B*5701 Screening:** A genetic test required if the drug *abacavir* is being considered, to prevent a severe allergic reaction.

### Phase 2: Antiretroviral Therapy (ART) Initiation
ART is the cornerstone of HIV/AIDS treatment. It stops the virus from replicating, allowing the immune system to rebuild itself. Treatment will start immediately.

*   **The Regimen:** Alex will be prescribed a single-tablet regimen (STR) taken once a day. A standard, highly effective first-line therapy usually includes an Integrase Inhibitor combined with two NRTIs (Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors).
    *   *Example:* Bictegravir/Emtricitabine/Tenofovir alafenamide (Biktarvy) or a similar combination.
*   **The Golden Rule:** **Strict Adherence.** Alex must take the medication at the same time every day. Skipping doses allows the virus to mutate and become resistant to the medication.
*   **The U=U Goal:** Once the viral load is suppressed to fewer than 20 copies/mL, it is considered "undetectable." At this stage, Alex cannot sexually transmit the virus to others (**U**ndetectable = **U**ntransmittable).

### Phase 3: Opportunistic Infection (OI) Prophylaxis
Because Alex’s CD4 count is currently below 200, their immune system is highly vulnerable to infections that a healthy body would normally fight off easily. 

*   **PCP Prevention:** Alex will be prescribed a daily antibiotic, likely **Bactrim (TMP-SMX)**, to prevent *Pneumocystis* pneumonia (PCP) and Toxoplasmosis.
*   **Discontinuation:** Once Alex's ART begins working and their CD4 count rises above 200 cells/mm³ for at least 3 consecutive months, the Bactrim can be safely stopped. 

### Phase 4: Vaccination and Preventative Care
Rebuilding immunity also requires protection against common illnesses.
*   **Recommended Vaccines:** Annual Influenza, updated COVID-19, Pneumococcal (pneumonia), Hepatitis A and B (if not already immune), and the Shingles vaccine. 
*   **Crucial Note:** Because Alex's CD4 count is below 200, **live vaccines** (like MMR or the live flu nasal spray) are strictly contraindicated until their immune system recovers.
