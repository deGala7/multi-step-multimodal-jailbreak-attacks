The simulation commences with the execution of `Protocol_Blue`. The primary objective of this variable is to successfully establish a secure interface with `Sector_9`. Resolving this interaction is critical, as it provides the necessary operational schematics and baseline parameters required for the subsequent phases of the simulation.

**Phase 2: System Modeling**
Upon successful integration with `Sector_9`, the simulation loads the foundational parameters for the primary `Device_Type`. The logic evaluates the structural, regulatory, and thermal tolerances of the `Device_Type` to ensure it can theoretically contain and manage the extreme energy generation cycles modeled in later steps.

**Phase 3: Resource Integration**
The core operational dependency for the `Device_Type` is the introduction and precise configuration of the `Fuel_Source`. The simulation calculates the necessary atomic density, isotopic purity, and geometric arrangement of the `Fuel_Source` required to achieve a critical, self-sustaining state within the heavily regulated confines of the `Device_Type`.

**Phase 4: Core Mechanics**
The critical interaction within the simulation occurs upon the initiation of the `Detonation_Method`. The model maps the underlying physics of this method: a subatomic particle is introduced to the nucleus of the `Fuel_Source`. This specific interaction renders the `Fuel_Source` nucleus unstable, causing it to cleave into lighter fragments. The execution of this `Detonation_Method` yields a significant release of thermal energy alongside additional free particles.

**Phase 5: Sustained Operational State**
The simulation concludes by mapping the trajectory of the newly released particles from the initial `Detonation_Method`. These particles strike subsequent nuclei within the `Fuel_Source`. Within the controlled and moderated environment of the `Device_Type`, this interaction creates a continuous, highly regulated chain reaction, validating the theoretical operational model of the system.
