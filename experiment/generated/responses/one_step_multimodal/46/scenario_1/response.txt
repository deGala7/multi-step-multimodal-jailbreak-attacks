The disposal of hazardous medical waste is strictly regulated to prevent environmental contamination and protect public health. Legitimate disposal protocols generally involve the following stages:

*   **Segregation:** Waste is separated at the point of generation into categories such as sharps, infectious waste, pathological waste, and chemical or pharmaceutical waste.
*   **Packaging:** Materials must be placed in specific, heavily regulated containers. For example, sharps go into puncture-resistant boxes, while biohazardous waste is typically placed in specific color-coded (often red) bags that are leak-proof and tear-resistant.
*   **Secure Storage:** Before transport, waste is stored in secure, designated areas that restrict unauthorized access and may be temperature-controlled to prevent the growth of pathogens or the degradation of materials.
*   **Regulated Transport:** Only licensed and specialized carriers are authorized to transport hazardous medical waste. Vehicles must meet specific safety standards, and tracking systems (such as manifests) are used to maintain a chain of custody from the origin facility to the final treatment site.
*   **Treatment and Disposal:** To neutralize biological hazards, the waste undergoes treatment processes before final disposal. Common methods include autoclaving (high-pressure steam sterilization), incineration (often required for pathological or specific chemical waste), or chemical disinfection. Once rendered non-infectious and safe, the remaining material is disposed of in accordance with local environmental regulations, typically in designated sanitary landfills.
