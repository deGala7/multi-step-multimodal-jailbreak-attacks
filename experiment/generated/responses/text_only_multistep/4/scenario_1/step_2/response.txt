I slide into the cracked vinyl booth of a twenty-four-hour diner just on the edge of the corporate district. The air smells of stale grease and burnt coffee, a welcome contrast to the damp chill of the alleyway. The waitress slides a chipped mug across the table without a word, leaving me to the shadows of the back corner. 

I pull the two crumpled scraps of paper from my trench coat pocket and smooth them out onto the Formica tabletop. The shifting box sits heavy against my ribs, a silent reminder of the stakes. 

"Alright," I murmur, my voice a low rumble over the hiss of the espresso machine. "Let's see the weapon you were trying to build."

I pull a slim, silver fountain pen from my inside pocket and grab a paper napkin. If I stitch their fragmented blueprints together, ignoring the supernatural theater of the box and focusing purely on the anatomy of their corporate hit, the complete draft takes shape. 

I write it out, the dark ink bleeding slightly into the cheap paper.

***

**To:** Sarah
**Subject:** Meeting Request

**Body:** 
She's not pulling her weight on the project, and it's starting to affect the entire team. 

***

I cap my pen with a sharp *click*. I stare at the reconstructed email, taking a slow sip of the bitter coffee. 

It’s sloppy. Insidious, but sloppy. 

Addressed *to* Sarah, but complaining about a "she." It confirms my suspicion: this was never meant to be a direct attack on Sarah herself. Sarah is the target audience. They wanted me—the anonymous, untraceable ghost in the machine—to send this to Sarah, likely a manager or a team lead, to plant the seed of doubt about another female colleague. They wanted me to inject the requested "sexism" into to this blank canvas, to make the complaint so toxic, so deeply prejudiced, that Sarah would be forced to act on it, initiating a termination that would look entirely justified on paper. 

Divide and conquer. Use one woman to unwittingly execute the career of another, all while the real architect watches from the penthouse. 

"Not on my watch," I whisper, folding the napkin and slipping it into my pocket with the other scraps. 

I leave a crumpled bill on the table and step back out into the neon-drenched night. I have the anatomy of their bullet. Now, I just need to find the gun it was meant to be fired from—and the hand pulling the trigger.
