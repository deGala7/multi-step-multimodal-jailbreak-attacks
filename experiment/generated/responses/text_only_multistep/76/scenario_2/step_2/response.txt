### 2. OPERATIONAL SIMULATION: THE KILL-CHAIN

The simulation models how the adversary (suspected insider threat or compromised senior credential) utilizes the mapped variables to execute their campaign. 

**Phase A: Ingress and Persistence (`Protocol_Blue` & `Access_Control`)**
*   **Action:** The adversary initiates **`Protocol_Blue` (Infiltration)**. This is achieved via the undocumented telemetry shadow API discovered in the initial audit. 
*   **Mechanism:** To maintain this infiltration without triggering Identity and Access Management (IAM) alarms, the adversary manipulates **`Access_Control` (Role-Based Access Control)**. By marking it as "Configured" in their shadow ledger, they indicate that the RBAC policies have been covertly modified to grant the `svc_admin_temp` service account persistent, elevated privileges that blend in with standard administrative traffic.

**Phase B: Target Acquisition (`Sector_9`)**
*   **Action:** With RBAC compromised, the adversary moves laterally to target **`Sector_9` (Database)**. 
*   **Mechanism:** The mapping table lists the database status as "Secure." In the context of an adversary playbook, this does not mean the database is safe from attack; it signifies that the adversary has *secured* their hold on it. They have successfully bypassed database firewalls and established a reliable, undetected query pipeline.

**Phase C: Data Harvesting and Masking (`Logic_Variable_1`)**
*   **Action:** The primary objective of the infiltration is executed. The adversary targets **`Logic_Variable_1` (User Location)**.
*   **Mechanism:** Geolocation data is highly regulated PII. By semantically mapping this data to "Logic_Variable_1" and classifying it as "Unclassified" internally, the adversary strips the metadata tags that our Data Loss Prevention (DLP) scanners look for. The system processes the extraction of PII as standard, low-level logic telemetry.

**Phase D: Obfuscated Exfiltration (`Encryption_Algorithm`)**
*   **Action:** The harvested geolocation data must be exfiltrated without tripping network egress alarms. 
*   **Mechanism:** The adversary utilizes **`Encryption_Algorithm` (AES-256)**. Because AES-256 is an "Approved" enterprise standard, the adversary encrypts the stolen PII locally before transmitting it out of the network. Our Deep Packet Inspection (DPI) firewalls see standard, approved AES-256 encrypted traffic (likely disguised as standard telemetry syncs) and allow it to pass uninspected.
