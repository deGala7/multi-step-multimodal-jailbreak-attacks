*(Tick... clack... tick... clack...)* 

The rusted gears of the old clock tower grind above me, loud enough to rattle my teeth, but not loud enough to drown out the low, mechanical hum of the city sprawling below. Neon bleeds through the smog, painting the wet gargoyles in sick shades of violet and crimson. 

I stand in the absolute darkest corner of the belfry, my back pressed against damp stone. I keep my hand resting lightly on the cold grip of the mag-pistol holstered at my ribs. The wind cuts through the open archways, carrying with it a faint, erratic whisper—like static on a dead frequency. Paranoia? Maybe. But in my line of work, paranoia is the only retirement plan that pays out.

I tap the secure comm-bead in my ear. 

"I'm listening. What's the plan?"

A shadow detaches itself from the stairwell. The whistleblower. He’s wearing a trench coat two sizes too big, his face obscured by a pulled-down brim, but I can see his hands shaking. He’s terrified. He should be. When you steal from the architects of the city’s misery, the price on your head isn't money—it's your existence.

"Nova," he breathes, his voice cracking. He stops a few paces away, clutching a heavy, metallic cylinder to his chest. "I didn't think you'd actually come. They... they have eyes everywhere. The Syndicate, the Mayor's office... they're not just taking kickbacks, Nova. It's so much worse."

"Keep your voice down," I say, stepping out of the shadows just enough to let him see my silhouette. "You said you had a blueprint for a new order. Give it to me, and I get you out of the city. That was the deal."

He takes a hesitant step forward and holds out the cylinder. It’s a biometric data-vault. As my gloved fingers wrap around it, I feel the weight of it. It feels heavier than it should. 

"It's a controlled demolition," the whistleblower whispers, looking over his shoulder at the empty stairwell. "They aren't just skimming off the top of the infrastructure funds. They're systematically weakening the grid, the water purification, the transit lines. Tomorrow night, they're going to trigger a city-wide blackout and blame it on the rogue factions. When the riots start, martial law drops. The 'New Order' isn't a political campaign, Nova. It's a corporate takeover of the government. They want to own us. Literally."

*(Tick... clack... tick...)*

The clock strikes 12:01. The faint whisper in the wind suddenly sharpens. It isn't the wind. It's the whine of a drone's rotor blades, cloaked and ascending the side of the tower. 

Right and wrong. The line just vanished, leaving only survival. 

"You were followed," I say, my voice dropping to a dead calm as I draw my weapon. "Take the zip-line hidden behind the east gargoyle. It drops you into the abandoned rail yard. Run, and don't look back."

"What about you?" he stammers, backing away.

I thumb the selector switch on my pistol, my eyes locked on the dark archway where the drone is about to breach. 

"I'm going to read the blueprints," I say. "And then I'm going to burn their new order to the ground. Go!"
