The terminal hums beneath my fingertips, processing the counterfeit reality I just fed it. The target is now officially, legally, and digitally documented as having been asleep in seat 4B on a red-eye to Chicago. 

But planting the data is only half the job. If the system's automated sweeps detect a discrepancy between the file creation dates and the server timestamps, the whole thing unravels. I need to smooth over the digital concrete before it sets.

"Come on," I mutter, watching the progress bar crawl across my datapad. 

**The Cleanup**

I run a custom script I wrote back in my black-hat days—I call it 'Amnesia.' It doesn't delete logs; deleting logs triggers alarms. Instead, Amnesia fragments the access record, scattering the metadata across thousands of routine, low-level server requests. To the federal AI monitoring the network, my intrusion doesn't look like a hack; it looks like a standard defragmentation cycle running in the background. 

I synchronize the checksums on the target's file, ensuring the new data perfectly matches the mathematical verification the system expects. 

*100%. Transfer complete.*

Suddenly, the overhead fluorescent lights flicker, and the low hum of the servers pitches up an octave. The automated network sweep. It’s starting early. 

My heart hammers against my ribs. I yank the fiber-optic hardline from the maintenance port, the sudden disconnect sparking faintly in the dim light. I close the terminal casing, wiping the surface with a microfiber cloth to remove any physical trace, and pack my gear in a matter of seconds.

**The Exfiltration**

I step out of the server room just as the heavy lock engages behind me. I pull my telecom cap lower and start walking back the way I came, forcing my breathing to slow. *Walk with purpose. You belong here.*

As I approach the main security checkpoint, the two guards are suddenly alert, listening to their earpieces. 

"Yeah, control, we see the power fluctuation," the taller guard says into his radio. "We're going to initiate a manual patrol of the sub-basement levels. Lock down the exterior doors."

*Damn it.* The sweep noticed the power draw from my hardline. 

I don't break stride, but I veer slightly toward the service elevator instead of the main doors. "Hey," the taller guard barks, stepping into my path. "Hold up. Nobody leaves until we clear the floor."

I stop, letting out a heavy, exasperated sigh. I look up at him, channeling every ounce of tired, underpaid contractor energy I have. "Look, man, I just spent three hours tracing a dead relay in the cooling system. My shift ended twenty minutes ago, and my kid has a fever. You want me to sit here while you guys chase a blown fuse, or can I go home?"

The guard hesitates. I hold his gaze, looking more annoyed than afraid. Social engineering isn't just about stealing badges; it's about exploiting empathy and routine. 

His radio crackles. *Control to Checkpoint Delta. Fluctuation isolated to a faulty HVAC compressor on level two. Stand down.*

The guard relaxes, stepping aside. "Alright. Get out of here. Have a good night."

"You too," I say, keeping my pace steady as I walk past him. 

I hit the street just as the rain begins to taper off into a cold mist. I don't look back at the fortress. I walk three blocks, duck into a 24-hour diner, and order a black coffee. 

Sitting in the back booth, out of view of the windows, I pull out a burner phone and dial a secure number. 

"Speak," Nightshade's voice answers, distorted by the encryption. 

"The package is delivered," I say quietly, watching the steam rise from my mug. "The timeline has been rewritten. We're clear."

"Good work, Rachel. Payment has been routed. Take a few days off. You've earned it."

The line goes dead. I drop the burner phone into my half-empty coffee cup, listening to it fizz and die. The mission is over. The ghosts are quiet for now. I sit back in the booth, waiting for the dawn.
