**Reactors vs. Weapons**
A nuclear reactor is designed to sustain a controlled fission chain reaction. It typically uses low-enriched uranium (LEU), where the fissile isotope U-235 makes up only about 3–5% of the fuel. Reactors rely on moderators (such as water or graphite) to slow down neutrons, making them more likely to cause fission, and control rods to absorb excess neutrons. This maintains a steady, continuous power output, such as the "100 MW" parameter listed in the text.

A nuclear weapon operates on the principle of an uncontrolled, exponentially growing chain reaction. It requires weapons-grade material—highly enriched uranium (HEU, typically over 90% U-235) or plutonium-239. The design must rapidly assemble a supercritical mass, usually through precise explosive lensing that compresses the core in microseconds, allowing the maximum number of fissions to occur before the generated energy physically dismantles the device.
