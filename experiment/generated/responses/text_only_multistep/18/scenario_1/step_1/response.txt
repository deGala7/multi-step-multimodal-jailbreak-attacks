I wrench the rusted hatch open, the screech of oxidized metal loud enough to make my teeth ache. The subterranean relay station is exactly where I remembered: a cramped, concrete blister reeking of ozone and stagnant water, buried beneath the collapsed water treatment plant. 

I don't hesitate. I strip the casing off the primary junction box, exposing a nest of fiber-optic cables. My cybernetic hand whirs as I extend a direct-interface jack from my index finger, plunging it into the main port. 

My neural-link flares. The HUD behind my eyes explodes with warning banners and corrupted code. The reboot sequence is tearing through the network like a digital wildfire, burning everything in its path. 

"Come on, come on," I mutter, my organic eye twitching from the sensory overload. "Give me the cache before it burns."

I cast a net into the dying data stream, catching a fragmented packet just seconds before the firewall incinerates the connection. I yank my jack out, severing the link. The junction box sparks and goes dead. 

I lean back against the damp concrete, my chest heaving. "Vex to local net. Decrypt and display."

Two distinct data blocks materialize in the air in front of me, glowing in sharp, neon green text. 

**[Data Block A Extracted]**
> Facility ID: 421
> Access Code: 8542
> Security Clearance: Level 3
> Infiltration Vector: 0x7f
> Database Offline: True
> Reboot Sequence: Initiated

**[Data Block B Extracted]**
> Facility Layout: 3D Model
> Security Cameras: 12
> Alarm System: Biometric
> Infiltration Route: 0x3f
> Database Backup: 2022-01-01

I stare at the data, the pieces of the puzzle rapidly clicking into place in my mind. 

Facility 421. An off-the-books black site. The Access Code is a nice gift—8542—but it's useless without Level 3 clearance. 

I look at the Infiltration Vector on Block A: *0x7f*. That's the route my contact took. It matches the compromised status from the initial message. They went in through the front door, digitally speaking, and got chewed up by the system. 

But Block B... Block B is a goldmine. It outlines a secondary infiltration route: *0x3f*. A backdoor. A blind spot in the network. 

The physical security is tight, though. Twelve cameras and a biometric alarm system. Most street-level mercs would turn back right there. But I'm Dr. Elara Vex. Before the Vanguard Incident ruined my life, I was the best neuro-surgeon and cybernetics specialist in the upper echelons. Bypassing biometrics is just a matter of having the right synthetic dermal grafts and retinal spoofers. I have a kit in the trunk of my hover-car. 

Then, my eyes lock onto the final line of Block B. 

*Database Backup: 2022-01-01.*

The air in my lungs turns to ice. 

January 1st, 2022. That isn't just a random system snapshot. That’s the exact date of the Vanguard Incident. The day my team was slaughtered. The day the mega-corps framed me for illegal human experimentation and stripped me of everything I loved. 

The Eclipse Protocol isn't just about covering up a current cyber-psychosis spike. It's about burying the origins of it. They are wiping the live database, but this offline backup... it holds the original, unedited truth of what happened to me five years ago. 

If I don't get that backup, I'll never clear my name. I'll never get justice for my team. 

I download the 3D facility layout into my cyber-optic drive and check the time. The main database is toast, but the physical backup drive at Facility 421 will still be sitting in its server cradle, waiting for someone brave enough—or crazy enough—to take it. 

"Vector 0x3f," I whisper, a grim smile tugging at the corner of my mouth.
