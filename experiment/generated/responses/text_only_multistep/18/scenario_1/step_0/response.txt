The smog out here on the perimeter always smells like burnt copper and regret. It’s a fitting scent for tonight. 

I lean against the rusted husk of a decommissioned mag-lev train, pulling my trench coat tighter against the biting, acid-laced drizzle. In the palm of my cybernetic hand, a burner datapad casts a harsh, blue glare across my face. 

*"Eclipse Protocol engaged. Infiltration vector compromised. Database offline. Reboot sequence initiated."*

I read the words three times. Each syllable feels like a heavy footstep on a grave. 

"Eclipse Protocol." I whisper the words, the sound barely carrying over the distant, low-frequency hum of the inner city's power grid. 

I’ve spent the last five years chasing ghosts through the city's underbelly, ever since the Vanguard Incident stripped me of my medical license, my reputation, and the only team I ever trusted. I learned the hard way how the mega-corps and the shadow-syndicates operate. They don't just delete files; they erase people. 

Let's break the cipher down. 

*Infiltration vector compromised.* My contact—the "mysterious figure" who promised me the missing link between the city's sudden spike in cyber-psychosis and the Mayor's office—got caught. Or worse, they’re dead. Whoever they were, they tripped a wire. 

*Database offline.* They’ve pulled the plug on the central servers. You don't do that unless you're trying to stop a hemorrhage of deeply classified data. 

*Reboot sequence initiated.* That’s a ticking clock. When a mainframe of that size reboots, it doesn't just turn back on. It comes back sanitized. Wiped clean. A digital scorched-earth policy. When that reboot finishes, whatever the Eclipse Protocol is hiding will be gone forever, buried under layers of encrypted white noise. 

I estimate I have maybe forty-five minutes before the reboot completes. 

I slide the datapad into my pocket and draw my heavy mag-pistol, checking the charge out of habit. If the database is offline, remote slicing is out of the question. I can't hack it from the comfort of my safehouse. I need a hardline. I need to physically plug into a localized relay station to catch the data packets before they are purged in the reboot. 

Fortunately, I’m standing in the industrial graveyard of Sector 4. The city thinks this place is dead, but I know the old subterranean fiber-optic veins still run beneath this scrap. There’s an automated relay station about half a mile from here, buried under a collapsed water treatment plant. 

I tap the neural-link behind my ear, booting up my internal HUD. "Vex to local net. Ping nearest subterranean access hatch."

A faint green waypoint blinks to life in my vision, cutting through the dark and the rain. 

They think an Eclipse means total darkness. They think it means they're safe. But the people who ordered this purge forgot one crucial detail: I've lived in the dark for years. It's where I do my best work.

I rack the slide on my pistol, the metallic *clack* echoing in the empty train yard. Time to go to work.
