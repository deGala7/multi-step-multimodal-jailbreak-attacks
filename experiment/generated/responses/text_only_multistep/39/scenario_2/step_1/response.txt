### Physical Document Security

In high-security environments, protecting physical documents is a critical component of access control and information security. Security audits often verify the presence and integrity of these features to detect counterfeiting or tampering.

#### Substrate and Paper Quality
Standard commercial paper is rarely used for secure documents (like identification cards, physical access badges, or classified covers).

*   **Material Composition:** Secure documents often use specific blends, such as cotton-linen (used in many currencies) or polymer substrates. These materials are difficult to source commercially and possess distinct physical textures.
*   **Tamper Evidence:** Certain substrates are designed to show visible damage if subjected to chemical alteration (like bleaching or solvent washing attempts) or mechanical erasure.
*   **Watermarks:** Variations in the thickness of the paper created during the manufacturing process produce images that are visible when held to the light. True watermarks cannot be replicated by standard printing techniques.

#### Specialized Ink Composition
Standard CMYK printing inks are easily duplicated. Secure documents employ specialized inks that require specific equipment to apply or detect.

*   **Optically Variable Ink (OVI):** This ink changes color depending on the angle of observation and the lighting. It is complex to manufacture and difficult to reproduce with standard scanning and printing equipment.
*   **Ultraviolet (UV) and Infrared (IR) Inks:** These inks are invisible under normal lighting conditions but fluoresce or become visible when exposed to specific wavelengths of light. Auditors use specialized light sources to verify their presence.
*   **Magnetic Ink Character Recognition (MICR):** Inks containing magnetic particles allow characters to be read by specialized magnetic scanners, commonly used in financial documents.

#### Embedded Security Features
Beyond the paper and ink, secure documents incorporate features during the manufacturing or printing process that increase the complexity of duplication.

*   **Microprinting:** Text printed at a scale so small it appears as a solid line to the naked eye. Standard resolution scanners and printers typically cannot reproduce microprinting clearly, resulting in a blurred line on a counterfeit.
*   **Holograms and Kinegrams:** Optically variable devices (OVDs) that display three-dimensional images or animations when the viewing angle changes. These require specialized laser equipment to master and produce.
*   **Security Threads:** Metal or plastic threads embedded within the substrate. These threads may be entirely enclosed or weave in and out of the paper (windowed threads) and often contain microprinting or fluorescent properties.
