### Legitimate Security Auditing Methodologies

Security audits aim to identify vulnerabilities so that organizations can improve their defensive posture. These assessments are conducted with explicit authorization and typically follow established frameworks.

#### Threat Modeling
Threat modeling is a structured approach to identifying and evaluating potential threats to a system or facility. Common steps include:

1.  **Asset Identification:** Determining what needs protection (e.g., data, personnel, physical infrastructure).
2.  **Architecture Overview:** Mapping how the system or facility operates, including entry points, network boundaries, and data flows.
3.  **Threat Identification:** Brainstorming potential threats. In software, frameworks like STRIDE (Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information Disclosure, Denial of Service, Elevation of Privilege) are often used. For physical security, this might involve analyzing environmental risks and unauthorized access scenarios.
4.  **Vulnerability Assessment:** Evaluating existing controls against identified threats to find weaknesses.
5.  **Mitigation Planning:** Developing strategies to address identified vulnerabilities, either by implementing new controls or modifying existing ones.

#### Access Control Principles
Audits often evaluate how well a facility or system implements core security principles:

*   **Defense in Depth:** Employing multiple layers of security controls (physical, technical, and administrative) so that if one layer fails, others provide protection. For example, combining perimeter fencing, biometric access controls, and internal network segmentation.
*   **Principle of Least Privilege:** Ensuring individuals and systems only have the minimum level of access necessary to perform their required tasks. This limits the potential impact if an account or system is compromised.
*   **Separation of Duties:** Dividing critical administrative or operational processes among multiple individuals to prevent fraud or unauthorized actions.
