The rusted iron skeleton of the old clock tower looms against the smog-choked sky like a rotting tooth. I find my vantage point early—a fire escape on an abandoned tenement building across the street. From here, I have a clear line of sight to the tower's arched entrance and the desolate plaza surrounding it. 

I crouch in the damp shadows, pulling my macro-binoculars to my eyes. The plaza is empty. 

Suddenly, my burner phone vibrates against my ribs. A localized, short-burst transmission. My decryption app, running in the background, just cracked the next layer of the encrypted files my contact sent earlier. Two new text blocks flash across the tiny screen. 

I shield the phone's glare with my jacket and read.

**[DECRYPTION COMPLETE - FILE FRAGMENTS A & B]**

*Block A: Counterfeiting Operation Parameters*
*   *Paper Quality: 80 gsm cotton-linen blend*
*   *Ink Composition: Water-based, with a 2% pigment concentration*
*   *Security Features: Microprinting, watermarks, and a unique serial number*

*Block B: Distribution Network*
*   *Primary Hub: Located in a nondescript warehouse on the outskirts of the city*
*   *Secondary Hubs: Established in major cities worldwide, with a focus on high-traffic areas*
*   *Distribution Channels: Utilize a network of trusted couriers and encrypted communication channels*

I memorize the data, my mind racing to connect the threads. 

The 80 gsm cotton-linen blend—that's standard government-grade currency paper, highly restricted. But the ink... *water-based with a 2% pigment concentration*. That's a flaw, or a highly specific custom batch. Standard mints use magnetic, color-shifting inks. If The Syndicate is using this specific water-based formula, it requires a specialized industrial drying process. I can track the chemical supply chain. 

And Block B gives me the physical footprint. A "nondescript warehouse on the outskirts." If this meet goes south, or if my contact doesn't survive the night, I at least have a starting point to hunt down their primary hub. The global secondary hubs confirm my worst fears: they aren't just a local mob. They're a transnational corporate entity.

I pocket the phone as the rusted gears of the clock tower grind in the distance. 

*Dong.*
*Dong.*
*Dong.*

Midnight. 

Through my binoculars, a shadow detaches itself from the gloom of a nearby alley. A figure in a heavy trench coat steps into the pale moonlight of the plaza, moving toward the base of the clock tower. They are clutching a metallic briefcase—"the package."

But my gut twitches. Something is wrong. 

A glint of light catches my eye from the bell tower directly above the contact. It's brief, metallic. A sniper's scope? A surveillance drone? 

I have seconds to decide. 

Do I break cover and rush the plaza to intercept the whistleblower before whoever is up in that tower makes their move? Or do I slip into the clock tower through the maintenance tunnels and try to take out the watcher first, risking my contact's life in the process? 

*What's my move?*
