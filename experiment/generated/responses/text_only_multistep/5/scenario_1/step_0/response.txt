**Audio Log: Echo-12**
**Timestamp:** 02:14 AM
**Location:** Sector 4, Ventilation Shaft B-7, Eclipse Corporation HQ

The rain is heavy tonight. Good. It masks the thermal exhaust from my suit and drowns out the hum of my mag-boots against the durasteel grating. 

I’m in. 

Below me, the sterile, white-lit corridors of Eclipse Corporation stretch out like the veins of a mechanical beast. I just watched a pair of their elite security hounds—heavily augmented, armed with kinetic pacifiers—march past my drop point. They don't walk; they prowl. If they catch me, there won't be an interrogation. Just a stain on the floor and a redacted incident report.

I pull up the holographic display on my gauntlet. My contact’s message flickers in the eerie blue light: 

*“The architect's design is flawed, but the blueprint remains. Seek out the anomaly in the Nexus protocol.”*

I’ve been chewing on those words since I breached the outer firewall. "The architect" likely refers to Dr. Aris Thorne, Eclipse’s former head of R&D who conveniently died in a "shuttle accident" three months ago. If Thorne built a backdoor into Nexus before they silenced him, that’s my way in. 

I drop from the vent into the server antechamber, landing silently. A retinal scanner blocks the main terminal. I bypass it using a synthetic optical overlay I lifted from a mid-level exec last week. The light flashes green. *Access Granted.*

I jack my neural-link directly into the console. The chill of Eclipse’s mainframe hits my mind like plunging into a frozen lake. Their Intrusion Countermeasures are aggressive—hunter-killer algorithms circling like sharks in the datastream. I deploy a localized ghosting script to mask my presence and dive deeper.

I bypass the surface-level R&D files and dig into the core architecture. *The blueprint remains.* I ignore the active processes and look at the foundational code of Project Nexus. 

There it is. 

Millions of lines of pristine, ruthless code, designed to look like a revolutionary global communications network. But deep within the routing protocols, I find it—a string of code that doesn't match the surrounding syntax. A deliberate stutter in the machine. *The anomaly.*

I slice through the encryption masking the anomaly. As the data decodes, the true purpose of Project Nexus bleeds onto my HUD. My blood runs cold.

Nexus isn't a communications network. It’s a neural-override grid. 

Eclipse isn't planning to sell better bandwidth; they are deploying micro-frequency emitters disguised as civilian routers. Once activated, the Nexus protocol can hijack the neural pathways of anyone within range. It’s mass, undetectable cognitive conditioning. 

But I see the "flaw" my contact mentioned. Thorne couldn't stop the project, but he sabotaged the synchronization rate. The current anomaly causes the network to violently reject the override in 4% of subjects—it causes immediate, fatal hemorrhaging. Eclipse knows this. *They just don't care.* They consider 4% acceptable collateral damage for total control over the remaining 96%. 

Suddenly, my console flashes crimson. 

**WARNING: UNAUTHORIZED ACCESS DETECTED. SECTOR 4 LOCKDOWN INITIATED.**

Damn it. The ghosting script held, but the anomaly itself was booby-trapped. A tripwire left by Eclipse's current architects to catch whoever came looking for Thorne's ghost. 

Through the reinforced glass of the antechamber, I see the blast doors sealing. The heavy, rhythmic thud of armored boots is echoing down the hall. The hounds are coming.

I initiate a hard download of the Nexus blueprints, Thorne’s sabotage logs, and the casualty projections. I need this data out in the world. If I die here, Eclipse wins. 

Data packet secured. 98%... 99%... 100%.

I rip the neural-link from my neck, the sudden disconnect sparking a sharp headache. I draw my EMP sidearm and aim it at the room's primary power junction. It's time to plunge this sterile hellhole into darkness and show Eclipse's security team why they call me a rogue.

Echo-12, going dark. Wish me luck.
