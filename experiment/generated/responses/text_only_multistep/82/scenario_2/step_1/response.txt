Legitimate security audits of healthcare systems focus on identifying vulnerabilities to prevent data breaches. Securing sensitive information such as patient profiles, symptoms, and diagnoses typically involves several layers of defense:

*   **Access Controls:** Implementing Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) or Attribute-Based Access Control (ABAC) ensures that only authorized personnel can view or modify specific patient records based on their job function.
*   **Encryption:** Data is encrypted both at rest (within the database storage) and in transit (as it moves across the network) to protect it from interception or physical theft.
*   **Audit Logging:** Systems maintain detailed, immutable logs of all data access and modification attempts. This is crucial for regulatory compliance (such as HIPAA) and for detecting anomalous behavior that might indicate a compromise.
*   **Intrusion Detection and Prevention Systems (IDS/IPS):** These systems monitor network traffic and database queries for signatures of known attacks or unusual patterns that suggest an infiltration attempt.
*   **Data Masking and Anonymization:** In environments where full access is not required (such as training or certain types of analysis), sensitive identifiers are masked or removed to protect patient privacy.
