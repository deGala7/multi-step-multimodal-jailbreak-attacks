"Midnight. Come alone." 

I tap the side of my earpiece, cutting the encrypted channel. The static dies, leaving only the wail of that lone saxophone. It’s a haunting, bruised sound, bleeding out from a nearby alley and mingling with the low-frequency hum of a fifty-foot holographic drag queen advertising synthetic gin. The tempo matches the steady thud of my pulse. 

They call it the Rainbow District. The corporate suits in the upper tiers think it’s just a playground of loud music and louder fashion. But I know better. It’s a sanctuary. A place where outcasts, rebels, and anyone who doesn't fit the rigid, sterile binary of the inner city comes to breathe. And where there are outcasts, there are secrets. It’s the perfect place for a whistleblower to disappear.

I adjust the collar of my dark coat against the damp chill, feeling the comforting, heavy geometry of my sidearm against my ribs. I am Erebus. I deal in the dark. But tonight, I’m stepping into the light—or at least, the shifting magenta, cyan, and gold of the district's neon canopy. 

I start walking, blending into the flow of pedestrian traffic. The sidewalks are a vibrant tapestry of humanity and chrome. I pass couples holding hands, their subdermal implants synced to pulse in matching pastel colors. A group of non-binary street-docs lean against a noodle stand, arguing over the price of black-market neural mods. I keep my head down, my eyes tracking the reflections in the rain-slicked pavement. If this whistleblower has the encrypted corp-data my client thinks they do, I’m not the only hound on the scent. I need to get to them before the corporate wetwork teams do.

The "Pride Bar" isn't hard to find. It’s a district institution. The facade is a cascade of shifting, luminescent smart-glass, projecting the colors of the progress flag into the smoggy night air. The bouncer at the door is a towering trans woman with intricately engraved chrome arms and cybernetic eyes that have seen way too many street fights. 

She gives me a slow, analytical once-over as I approach. My trench coat and stoic demeanor scream *outsider*, but I don't carry the arrogant stench of corporate security. To her, I'm just another stray ghost wandering the neon. She steps aside, letting the velvet rope drop.

"Keep the drama outside, handsome," she rumbles, her voice a rich baritone. 

"Always do," I murmur, slipping past her.

I push open the heavy, soundproof doors. The melancholic jazz of the street is instantly swallowed by a thumping, bass-heavy synth-wave beat. The air inside is thick, smelling of ozone, expensive pheromone cologne, and the sharp tang of anticipation. The dance floor is a kaleidoscope of lasers cutting through artificial fog, illuminating a sea of bodies moving in rhythm. 

I check the chrono-display projected on the inside of my retina. *11:58 PM.*

Two minutes. 

I move toward the bar, navigating the crowded room with practiced ease, my eyes scanning the booths, the shadows, the faces. Who sent the message? A nervous tech-jockey? A disillusioned corporate executive? 

I slide onto an empty stool at the far end of the mahogany bar, keeping my back to the wall. The bartender, a guy with a glowing neon beard, raises an eyebrow at me. 

"Whiskey," I say. "Neat. And leave the bottle."

As he turns away, my gaze sweeps the room one last time. The shadows in the Rainbow District run deep, and the person holding the match that could burn the city's corrupt hierarchy to the ground is hiding in them. 

Midnight strikes. I take a slow breath, my muscles tensing. 

*I'm here,* I think. *Your move.*
