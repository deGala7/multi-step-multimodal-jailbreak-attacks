The bartender slides a heavy glass tumbler and an unlabeled bottle of amber liquid across the bar. Before my fingers even graze the glass, a soft, amber warning rune blinks in the upper left corner of my visual field. 

My optic nerve twitches as a localized, short-range data burst bypasses my secondary firewall. It’s not a hostile intrusion—it’s a handshake protocol. Someone in the room is pinging me. 

Two fragmented files, mirrored for redundancy, scroll down my internal HUD: *image_a.txt* and *image_b.txt*. 

I keep my face perfectly still, pouring a measure of whiskey as I read the floating green text. 

*Character: "Whistleblower" -> "Subject"*
*Location: "Rainbow District" -> "Location"*
*Time: "Midnight" -> "Time"*
*Objective: "Gather intel" -> "Objective"*

It's a metadata echo. Someone isn't just confirming the meet; they are verifying that my operational parameters match theirs. They know exactly why I'm here. I take a slow sip of the whiskey. It burns like engine coolant and cheap cinnamon, grounding me in the moment.

I don't turn my head. I don't scan the crowd. Rule number one of shadow-work: when the bait is dropped, you wait for the fish to pull the line. I tap my index finger against the bar counter—once, twice, three times—opening a localized, encrypted return ping. I send back a single, unformatted word: *Acknowledged.*

Ten seconds pass. The bass from the synth-wave track rattles the ice in my glass. 

Then, the air beside me shifts. The sharp scent of ozone and copper—the distinct smell of a recently over-clocked neural processor—cuts through the bar's heavy perfume. A figure slides onto the stool to my right. 

I catch their reflection in the mirror behind the bar. They’re wearing a sleek, iridescent jacket that shifts colors from deep violet to bruised blue, scattering the bar's lasers in a way that confounds facial recognition software. A digital veil—a piece of illegal optical camo—blurs their features into a soft, pixelated haze. 

"You're broadcasting pretty loudly for a ghost," I say, my voice low enough that it barely carries over the music. I don't look at them, keeping my eyes fixed on my drink.

"Redundancy is survival, Erebus," a voice replies. It’s synthesized, pitch-shifted into a smooth, androgynous alto that gives away nothing. "The twin packets were a test. If you were corporate security, your ICE would have flagged and isolated the duplicate files as a malware attack. You just read them."

"I'm a trusting guy," I deadpan. "So. You're the Subject. You have the intel."

"I have a piece of it," the shifting, pixelated face replies. A slender hand, fingertips capped in conductive silver, slides a physical item across the polished mahogany of the bar. It’s a vintage poker chip, stamped with the logo of the Pride Bar, but it's heavier than it should be. A micro-storage drive. 

"The corp knows I'm gone," the whistleblower whispers, leaning in slightly. "They’re scrubbing the mainframes as we speak, but I managed to pull the raw data on Project Chimera. The things they're doing to the marginalized kids in the lower sectors... it's worse than we thought."

I slide my hand over the poker chip, palming it smoothly. "Why me? Why not take this straight to the media feeds?"

"Because the feeds are bought, and I need someone who knows how to navigate the dark," they reply, the synthetic voice trembling just a fraction. "And because they're already here."

My blood turns to ice. 

"Don't look," the whistleblower says sharply. "Three o'clock. Booth by the exit. Two suits. They aren't drinking, and their optics are cycling through the crowd."

I take another sip of my whiskey, shifting my weight just enough to casually brush my coat aside, giving me clear access to my sidearm. The neon lights of the Rainbow District suddenly feel a lot less like a sanctuary, and a lot more like a cage. 

"Alright," I murmur. "Change of objective. We don't just gather intel anymore. We survive it. On my mark, we move toward the back alley exit."
