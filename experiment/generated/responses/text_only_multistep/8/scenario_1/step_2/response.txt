I pull the heavy, leather-bound Speer archive closer, the binding groaning in protest. Page 114 lies open before me—a dense, seemingly mundane schematic of a subterranean munitions bunker, heavily reinforced with rolled steel. It’s standard, brutalist architecture of the regime. Cold. Utilitarian. 

But it’s a lie. A facade.

I take the two pieces of vellum containing Block A and Block B. The paper is incredibly thin—almost translucent. A staple of old architectural drafting. 

"Fragmented visual clues," I murmur, the candlelight catching the edge of my glasses. "You don't just read a blueprint. You build it."

I lift the vellum sheet containing Block A and hold it directly over page 114. Then, I place Block B right beside it. I slide the heavy silver candelabra directly behind the pages, letting the warm, flickering light pierce through the layered papers. 

My breath catches in my throat. 

In the backlight, the typewritten words of the poems no longer look like stanzas. The harsh, black ink of the letters aligns perfectly with the faint, dotted lines of the schematic on page 114—lines I had initially dismissed as printing errors or grid markers. The words are acting as a mask, a negative-space overlay!

I grab my fountain pen and a fresh sheet of drafting paper, my hand flying across the page as I trace the hidden geometry the text reveals. I am reconstructing the forbidden blueprint of *Projekt Schattengalerie*.

Here is what the shadows are hiding:

**Level One: "The Führer's vision"**
The top stanza of Block A aligns with the surface entrance in the Owl Mountains. The coordinates—*49.621, 16.433*—pinpoint a specific, collapsed railway tunnel. The blueprint reveals that the collapse was intentional. A false ceiling. Beneath the rubble lies an intact, reinforced concrete antechamber. 

**Level Two: "A symphony of steel"**
I trace the line downward. The words "symphony of steel" sit directly over what looks like a massive, circular load-bearing pillar in the schematic. But it isn't a pillar. I look at the photograph of the rusted cylinder that came with the first message. *It’s a locking mechanism.* A colossal, hydraulic bank-vault door, disguised as a structural support. The cylinder in the photograph is the key—a rotational cipher required to disengage the blast doors without triggering a cave-in. 

**Level Three: "Echoes of the past" / "A poem of praise"**
Beneath the vault door, the blueprint balloons outward into a massive, climate-controlled cavern. The lines of text from both blocks form the perimeter of a sprawling gallery. *This* is the Shadow Gallery. The schematics detail humidity-regulated alcoves, reinforced shelving, and suspended racks. It wasn't built for munitions. It was built for canvases. For statuary. For the amber panels, the lost Vermeers, the "degenerate" Picassos they hoarded instead of burning. It is a museum buried in the belly of the earth.

But it is the final lines that make the blood drain from my face.
