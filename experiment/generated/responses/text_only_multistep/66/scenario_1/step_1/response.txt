I pulled another sheet from the envelope, this one a crumpled, typewritten page that smelled faintly of damp earth and darkroom chemicals. I smoothed it out on the desk next to my Glock. 

Block A and Block B. Scene notes. Casting descriptions. 

I traced my finger over the first line in Block A: *Humans and animals in a suggestive pose, but no explicit content.* 

A dark smirk tugged at the corner of my mouth. There it is. The Director isn't just a butcher; he’s a psychological sadist. He knows that outright smut or gore is cheap. But *implication*? Skirting the edges of taboo, blurring the line between primal instinct and human depravity? That gets under the skin. It lodges in the brain and festers. 

Then we have the players. *'JEN (28), a free-spirited artist.'* Int. Bedroom - Night. 
And *'MAX (35), a rugged outdoorsman.'* Ext. Woods - Day. 

Followed by a note about a passionate embrace. 

On the surface, it reads like a pitch for a dime-store romance or a pretentious indie flick. But in the context of the abattoir blueprints I just looked at? It’s a trap. The Director is establishing a baseline of humanity and intimacy, probably just to violently deconstruct it. You can't shatter a mind if you don't build up its hopes and desires first.

I tapped my pen against the desk, the rhythm mirroring the ticking of the clock on my wall. 

"Jen" and "Max." Are these characters? Or are they targets? 

In the underground snuff and extreme-horror circles, directors rarely hire professional actors. They use "unwitting participants." They put out vague casting calls for experimental student films, or worse, they stalk real people who fit the archetype, drug them, and drop them onto the set when the cameras roll. If Jen is a real artist and Max is a real outdoorsman, they might not even know they've been cast yet.

The woods. The bedroom. The slaughterhouse. It's a three-act structure. Act One: The primal seduction in the wild. Act Two: The intimacy of the bedroom, tainted by the voyeuristic, animalistic undertones. Act Three: The abattoir. The grand finale.

I grabbed my laptop and cracked my knuckles. 

"Alright, Director," I muttered to the empty room. "Let's look at your casting process."

I opened up a secure browser and started scraping the local dark web boards, Craigslist casting calls, and underground fetish forums. I’m looking for any recent ads seeking a 28-year-old female artist or a 35-year-old outdoorsy male for an "unconventional," "experimental," or "high-paying" film project. 

If I can find Jen or Max before their call time, I can derail this production. If they're already missing... well, then I know exactly who I'm looking for when I kick the doors of that abattoir off their rusty hinges. 

*(I glance back at the typewritten page, my eyes lingering on the animalistic note)*

He's playing with the beast inside us. Let's see how he likes it when a real predator walks onto his set.
