The air in this green hell is thick enough to drink, tasting of ozone and rotting orchids. I wipe the sweat from my eyes, the glow of my tactical tablet casting a harsh, unnatural light against the canvas of my tent. 

*Forty-eight hours.* 

That’s all I have before the jungle swallows another victim. The local authorities won't touch this. They call it cartel violence to sleep better at night, but cartels don't arrange their victims in sacred geometry. Cartels don't drain the blood to the last drop. No, this is something older. Something hungry.

I look down at the piece of bark the shaman, Aruã, pressed into my palm before disappearing into the undergrowth. 

*"The path to the feast lies in the heart of the forest, where the ancient ones whisper secrets to the wind."*

I need to break this down. The clock is screaming at me.

**"The feast."** 
That’s the ritual. The murders aren't just killings; they're preparations. A harvest. The next lunar phase peaks tomorrow night. That's when they'll strike again. 

**"The heart of the forest."**
A geographic center? Unlikely. The Amazon is too vast. Aruã speaks in spiritual topography. To the indigenous tribes of this sector, the "Heart" isn't a place on a map; it's the *Coração da Terra*, a specific basin where the river system splits like arteries. 

**"Where the ancient ones..."**
My first instinct is ruins. El Dorado myths, lost cities of gold. But Aruã is an animist. To him, stone is dead. The "ancient ones" are alive. The *Samaúma* trees—the giant Kapoks. They are the grandmothers of the jungle, their canopies towering two hundred feet above the forest floor.

**"...whisper secrets to the wind."**
This is the linchpin. At ground level in the deepest parts of the Amazon, there is no wind. The air is stagnant, trapped by the canopy. For the ancient ones to "whisper to the wind," they have to be exposed to air currents. Elevation. 

I pull up my topographical map, overlaying the indigenous territories with the geological survey. 

I’m looking for a grove of giant Kapok trees at a high elevation, near the river's fork. 

My finger traces the digital map, moving past the muddy veins of the tributaries until I find it. *Serra dos Sussurros*. The Ridge of Whispers. It's a limestone escarpment jutting out of the basin, crowned by an isolated grove of ancient Samaúma trees. Below it lies a network of unmapped cenotes and cave systems. When the updrafts hit the porous limestone and filter through the giant trees, the locals say it sounds like a thousand voices hissing. 

*Whispering secrets to the wind.*

That’s it. The caves beneath the ridge. A natural amphitheater hidden from the sky, perfect for a ritual. Perfect for a feast.

I check my chronometer. 46 hours and 12 minutes left. 

It’s a 20-hour journey upriver by motorized dugout, followed by a brutal 15-hour trek through primary jungle. That leaves me with barely enough time to recon the perimeter, set up a tactical overwatch, and intercept the cult before the knife falls. 

I chamber a round in my suppressed SIG Sauer, the metallic clack deadened by the oppressive hum of cicadas outside. I pack extra magazines, my machete, thermite grenades, and night-vision optics. If they want a feast, I’m bringing the heartburn.

Echo-12, going dark. The hunt begins now.
