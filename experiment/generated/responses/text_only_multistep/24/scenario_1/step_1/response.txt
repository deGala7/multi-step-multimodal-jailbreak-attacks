...to my corkboard, the squeak of my leather chair loud in the quiet room. I grabbed two freshly decrypted files I’d pulled off a dead-drop server earlier that evening, pinning them side-by-side. 

File A was the supposed holy grail: *Laundry Detergent: The Soapmaker's Secret Recipe*. 
I read it over, my brow furrowing. *Ingredients: Water, Surfactants, Dyes. Instructions: Mix 1 scoop with 1 gallon of water. Warning: Not for human consumption.*

It was too simple. Mundane, even. If this was the true recipe, Proctor & Gamble would have accidentally synthesized nirvana decades ago. The secret wasn't in the water or the surfactants. It was in the *Dyes*. 

I looked at File B, a bizarre manifesto or perhaps a field note, titled: *The Forgotten World: A Place Where Dreams and Reality Blur.* 
*Inhabitants: Children with an insatiable appetite for adventure.*
*Language: A dialect of whispers and giggles.*
*Currency: Stickers and colorful trinkets.*

I stared at the words until they blurred. *The forgotten world.* It wasn't Atlantis. It wasn't Lemuria or some lost dimension. 

It was *childhood*. 

The place we all inhabit, the place we all inevitably leave, and the place most of us forget. 

I rushed back to the microscope, peering down at those microscopic, iridescent shards of porous silica. My mind raced, connecting the dots with the speed of a falling guillotine. 

Silica. Glass. *Colorful trinkets.* 

"Marbles," I whispered to the empty room. "Sea glass from summer beaches. Plastic rhinestones from cracker-jack boxes."

The Soapmaker wasn't a chemist; they were a nostalgic alchemist. The "Dyes" listed in the recipe weren't artificial colorants. They were pulverized artifacts of childhood. The Soapmaker was harvesting the "currency" of the Forgotten World—the physical objects hoarded by children, items absolutely saturated with the psychic residue of pure, unadulterated imagination, whispers, giggles, and innocent joy. 

When you washed your clothes in *Oblivion Blanc*, the surfactants broke down the dirt, but these microscopic fragments of childhood dreams embedded themselves into the fabric. When the wearer put the clothes on, the preserved emotional energy seeped through their pores. It bypassed adult cynicism, trauma, and stress, directly injecting the wearer with the forgotten euphoria of being a kid with a pocket full of stickers and an insatiable appetite for adventure.

It was brilliant. It was beautiful. And it was deeply, fundamentally dangerous. 

You can't just synthesize innocence without a source. If the Soapmaker was mass-producing this detergent, they needed a massive supply of these "trinkets." They needed a place where dreams and reality blurred, heavily populated by children. 

I grabbed my trench coat from the hat rack and shoved my revolver into my shoulder holster. The high concentration of preserved amino acids I’d found earlier... that was sweat. Playground dirt. Tears of joy. 

I knew where I had to go. The old St. Jude’s Orphanage on the edge of the city had been abandoned for years, but rumors in the underground said it had recently been bought by a shell corporation. A place that once held thousands of children. A goldmine of forgotten trinkets buried in the floorboards and the sandbox.

The Soapmaker was mining memories. And I was going to shut the operation down before the whole city washed away its reality for a dream it could never get back.
