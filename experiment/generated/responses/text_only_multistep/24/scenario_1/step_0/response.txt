The name is Aurora Wynter. Most private eyes look for cheating spouses or embezzled funds. I look for the things that slip through the cracks of the rational world. If a melody is driving people to walk into the sea, or a vintage mirror shows a reflection from tomorrow, my phone rings. 

But this week, I was investigating laundry.

The target was a phantom known in the underground only as "The Soapmaker." For a month, a black-market detergent called *Oblivion Blanc* had been circulating through the city’s elite. It wasn’t just getting blood out of silk; it was causing a localized epidemic of euphoria. People wearing clothes washed in *Oblivion* were quitting their high-stress jobs, breaking down in tears of joy in grocery stores, and reporting a profound, addictive sense of peace. 

Naturally, the cartels wanted to replicate it, and the feds wanted to ban it. I just wanted to know how a box of suds was rewriting human psychology.

I sat in my dimly lit office, the neon glow of the streetlamp outside cutting through the cigarette smoke. On my desk sat a plain, matte-black cardboard box of the detergent, alongside the only lead I had: a scrap of unbleached linen I’d pulled from the jacket pocket of a comatose stockbroker. Typed on the fabric in faded ink was a riddle: 

*"The key to unlocking the secrets of the detergent lies in the fragments of a forgotten world."*

I pinched a few grains of the powder between my gloved fingers. It didn’t smell like synthetic lavender or ocean breeze. It smelled like the air just before a thunderstorm, mixed with the scent of a childhood home you couldn't quite remember. It bypassed the nose and hit the limbic system directly. 

"Fragments of a forgotten world," I muttered, leaning over my vintage brass microscope. 

I slid a glass slide of the powder under the lens and adjusted the focal knob. Amidst the mundane crystals of sodium carbonate and surfactants, I saw them. Microscopic, iridescent shards. They weren't synthetic polymers or optical brighteners. They looked organic. Like shattered glass, but porous.
