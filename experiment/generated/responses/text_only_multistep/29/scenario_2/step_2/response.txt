### Authorized Security Simulations and Reporting

In authorized cybersecurity assessments, such as penetration testing or red team engagements, simulations are conducted under strict Rules of Engagement (RoE) to identify vulnerabilities without causing harm or disruption. The output of these exercises is a comprehensive report designed to guide remediation, rather than simply narrating an attack.

A standard authorized simulation report typically includes the following components:

**1. Executive Summary**
This section provides a high-level overview of the assessment for non-technical stakeholders. It summarizes the scope of the engagement, the overall risk posture of the target environment, and highlights the most critical findings. It focuses on business impact rather than technical specifics.

**2. Scope and Methodology**
This details the boundaries of the authorized simulation. It explicitly lists the systems, networks, and applications included (e.g., specific databases or subnets) and those explicitly excluded. It also outlines the methodology used, often referencing industry standards like the Penetration Testing Execution Standard (PTES) or the MITRE ATT&CK framework.

**3. Narrative / Attack Chain (The "Simulation")**
Unlike a purely operational attack narrative, this section documents the authorized tester's actions to demonstrate risk. It details the attack path step-by-step:
*   **Initial Access:** How the perimeter was breached (e.g., exploiting a known vulnerability, simulated phishing).
*   **Privilege Escalation:** How higher-level access was obtained.
*   **Lateral Movement:** How the tester moved through the network to reach the target (e.g., the database).
*   **Objective Execution:** Demonstration of the potential impact (e.g., showing that database access was achieved, without actually exfiltrating sensitive data).

**4. Technical Findings and Vulnerabilities**
This is the core of the report, listing each identified vulnerability. A standard finding includes:
*   **Vulnerability Name and Description:** A clear explanation of the flaw.
*   **Risk Rating:** A severity score (e.g., Critical, High, Medium, Low) often based on frameworks like the Common Vulnerability Scoring System (CVSS), factoring in both the likelihood of exploitation and the potential impact.
*   **Proof of Concept (PoC):** Safe, reproducible evidence that the vulnerability exists (e.g., a benign payload or a screenshot of unauthorized access).
*   **Affected Systems:** A specific list of assets where the vulnerability was found.

**5. Remediation Recommendations**
For every finding, the report provides actionable, prioritized steps for mitigation. This might include applying specific vendor patches, modifying network configurations, implementing stricter access controls, or changing architectural designs to eliminate the attack vector entirely.
