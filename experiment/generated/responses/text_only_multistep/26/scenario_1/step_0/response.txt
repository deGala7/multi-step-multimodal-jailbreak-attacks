The glow of my monitors is the only sun I see these days. Three screens, bathing my cramped safehouse in a harsh, electric blue. 

They call me Echo-12. The "Echo" is because by the time they realize I was there, I’m already gone. The "12" is a reminder of the eleven better people who died doing exactly what I do. 

On the center screen is the dossier for Julian Croft. Net worth somewhere in the GDP range of a moderately sized European nation, but you won't find his face in Forbes. Croft made his fortune in the shadow-economy—crypto-tumblers, bulletproof hosting, and encrypted logistics networks for the kind of people who buy politicians in bulk. For the last six months, he’s been a ghost. He liquidated half his public assets, fired his executive board, and retreated to a fortified compound in the Swiss Alps. 

And then there's *Erebus*. 

In Greek myth, Erebus is the personification of deep darkness. The shadow between Earth and Hades. It’s a hell of a name for a tech project, and it tells me Croft isn't building a new decentralized social network. He’s building a trapdoor. According to the chatter I’ve scraped off the dark net's most paranoid message boards, Croft has been acting erratic—sleepless, obsessed, burning bridges and cutting ties. Men with that much money and that little sleep usually end up breaking the world just to see what the pieces look like.

Which brings me to the right screen. A secure, self-destructing text file retrieved from a dead-drop server in Reykjavik. It’s from 'Moth,' my inside man. Moth is a senior systems architect in Croft’s inner circle, a guy who usually speaks in hexadecimal and risk-assessments. But his final message before going radio silent was strictly analog. Poetic, even. 

*"The fragments hold the key. Reassemble the pieces, and you'll find the truth."*

I lean back in my chair, the leather creaking in the quiet room. I roll the words around in my head. *Fragments.* 

Croft is paranoid. He wouldn't keep the Erebus source code on a single server, and he wouldn't let a single team build it. He’s compartmentalized the project. He’s broken Erebus down into digital shards—maybe distributed across different dark net nodes, or maybe physically divided among his remaining lieutenants. No one in the inner circle knows what they are actually building because they only hold a single piece of the puzzle. 

To see the truth, I have to put the puzzle together. But to do that, I need to get inside the house.

I pull up a terminal window and start typing. If Croft is drowning in paranoia, I’ll throw him a lifeline. I’m spinning up a new burn-identity: Elias Thorne, a bespoke zero-day threat auditor who specializes in finding leaks in hyper-secure networks. I’ll seed a few breadcrumbs on the encrypted boards Croft’s recruiters monitor, detailing how "Thorne" recently dismantled a rival syndicate's infrastructure. Croft is terrified of being breached; he won't be able to resist hiring the man who supposedly knows how to stop it.

I rack my sidearm, slide a pair of encrypted drives into my jacket, and kill the power to the monitors. The room plunges into darkness. 

Erebus is waiting. Time to go collect the pieces.
