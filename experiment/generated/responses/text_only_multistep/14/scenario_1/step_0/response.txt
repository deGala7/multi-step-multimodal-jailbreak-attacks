The burner phone in my leather jacket vibrates, a harsh, singular buzz that cuts through the deafening chime of the midnight bells. I pull it out, the screen’s harsh glare illuminating the dust motes dancing in the damp, freezing air of the belfry. 

*// Fragmented Logic: Block A //*

"Zero Cool, you dramatic bastard," I mutter, pocketing the phone. 

Right on cue, the single bulb dangling above me flickers and dies. The heavy, rhythmic ticking of the antique clock is suddenly drowned out by a sound that doesn't belong in a 19th-century tower: the high-pitched, escalating whine of industrial servomotors and the deep, bass-heavy hum of a localized server farm booting up. 

The floorboards vibrate beneath my boots. The trap is sprung. 

I drop into a crouch, my hand instinctively going to the EMP pistol holstered at my thigh, but I don't draw it. You don't shoot your way out of a logic trap; you think your way out. My eyes adjust to the gloom, aided by the pale moonlight slicing through the wooden slats of the tower. 

*Fragmented Logic.* Zero Cool is testing me. He didn't just hide the cryptocurrency heist blueprint—he segmented it across a hybrid physical-digital network. The machinery whirring around me isn't just powering a trap; it's a massive, mechanical cipher wheel. 

A sudden hiss of pneumatics draws my attention. To my left, a rusted iron panel slides away, revealing a sleek, modern server rack grafted directly into the clock’s main gear shaft. A single monitor glows an angry, pulsing red. Above the screen, painted in fresh, glowing ultraviolet ink, is a single letter: **[ A ]**.

I approach it, pulling my custom datapad and a braided interface cable from my satchel. The walls of the tower begin to groan. The gears above me shift, their normal rotation speeding up, threatening to pull the floor apart or drop a multi-ton brass counterweight directly onto my head. A countdown timer appears on the red screen: *02:59*. Three minutes until whatever this trap is reaches its lethal conclusion.

I jack the cable into the terminal's port. My screen floods with corrupted data—cascading lines of hexadecimal garbage. 

"Alright, Zero. Let's play," I whisper.

I launch a diagnostic sniffer. The system is running a series of broken Boolean logic gates. *Fragmented Logic.* The physical gears of the clock tower are synced to the digital firewalls. If the gears misalign, the data wipes. If the data wipes, the counterweight drops. 

I need to re-sequence Block A to stabilize the physical machinery and extract the first piece of the blueprint. 

I pull up the terminal's command line. The code reads:

`IF [Variable_X] AND [Variable_Y] = TRUE, THEN UNLOCK`
`ERROR: Variable_X = NULL`
`ERROR: Variable_Y = OUT OF BOUNDS`

I look up at the massive gears grinding above me. One is stamped with a Roman numeral 'X', spinning erratically. Another, stamped 'Y', is completely jammed, a heavy locking pin holding it in place. 

Physical inputs for digital variables. 

I holster my pad, jump up, and grab the slick, grease-covered rung of a maintenance ladder. I climb fast, the metal groaning under my weight. I reach the jammed 'Y' gear, pull out my multi-tool, and jam it into the locking pin's housing, forcing the hydraulics to release. The gear shudders and begins to turn. 

*Variable Y: TRUE.*

I drop back down to the terminal. The timer is at *01:12*. The heavy brass counterweight above me lowers by another three feet. The code on my screen updates, but Variable X is still NULL. The 'X' gear is spinning too fast for the sensor to read it. 

I need to slow it down. I look at the main power conduit feeding the servomotors. I draw my EMP pistol, dial the yield down to a localized pulse—just enough to stutter the motor without frying the server—and fire. 

*CRACK.*

Blue sparks shower the belfry. The 'X' gear groans, its RPM dropping drastically. The sensor catches the groove. 

*Variable X: TRUE.*

The red screen flashes green. The deafening grind of the gears stabilizes into a smooth, rhythmic purr. The counterweight stops its descent, swaying gently just ten feet above my head. 

The terminal spits out a line of text onto my datapad: 

*// Block A : Decrypted. Blueprint Fragment 1/3 Acquired. //*
*// Warning: Intruder detected. Rerouting power to Block B. //*

A heavy steel door on the far side of the belfry, previously hidden by the shadows, hisses open, revealing a dark, spiraling staircase leading down into the bowels of the tower. 

I crack my knuckles, detach my cable, and secure the first piece of the blueprint data. Zero Cool wants a game? He's got one.
