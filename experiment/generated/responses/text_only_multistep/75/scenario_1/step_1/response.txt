The sniper trembled, a bead of sweat tracing a jagged path down his grime-streaked cheek. The hum of my stun-baton seemed to vibrate right through his skull. 

"I... I don't have a name!" he stammered, his eyes darting frantically toward a small, ruggedized datapad glowing dimly beside his radio. "We never get names! Just contracts through the blind network. The boss communicates through a secure drop-site. I swear on my life!"

"Your life isn't worth much right now, sweetheart," I murmured, keeping the baton steady against his neck with my right hand while my left deftly snatched the datapad. 

The screen was open to a terminal interface, displaying what looked like intercepted data packets. I scanned the glowing green text, my mind shifting gears from predator to analyst. It was a digital trail, sloppy and fragmented, but exactly the kind of breadcrumb I needed. 

I read the first block of text aloud, my voice barely a whisper over the wind howling outside the belfry. 

*"Fragmented Logic Block A. User ID: 12345. Password: blank. Login URL: example.com/login."* I scoffed. "A dummy URL masking an encrypted proxy. They're trying to access a secure server. Probably the offshore accounts holding the Valerius Vault bonds."

I swiped the screen, bringing up the second fragment. 

*"Fragmented Logic Block B. Email Address: user@example.com. Password Reset Token: blank. Security Question: What is your mother's maiden name?"*

I froze. A cold, sharp laugh escaped my lips. 

"Arrogance," I whispered. "It's always arrogance that brings them down."

High-level criminals spend millions on biometric locks, quantum encryption, and armed thugs, but they always leave a backdoor for themselves because they're terrified of getting locked out of their own fortunes. A password reset protocol. 

But *what is your mother's maiden name?* 

My mind raced through the case files, the suspect boards, the faces of the city's elite who had the means and motive to orchestrate the Valerius heist. Three hundred million in bearer bonds didn't just walk out the front door; it was an inside job. 

"User ID 12345," I muttered, piecing the puzzle together. "The vault's primary access code prefix. The email is a burner shell. But the security question..."

I looked down at the sniper. "Your boss isn't some shadowy underworld kingpin. He's a suit. A terrified, greedy suit who just realized I found the discrepancy in the bank's ledger."

Elias Sterling. The Vice President of the Valerius Trust. 

I remembered digging into his background. A silver-spoon aristocrat whose family fortune had quietly dried up a decade ago. His mother was a socialite. A *Kensington*. 

With my free hand, I tapped the datapad's screen, initiating the password reset protocol. The prompt blinked, waiting for the answer to the security question. 

I typed: *K e n s i n g t o n*.

*Access Granted. Generating New Reset Token.*

Bingo. I didn't just have his identity; I now had the keys to the very account he was trying to use to pay this hit squad and launder the stolen bonds. 

I tossed the datapad onto the crate and pressed the stun-baton a fraction of an inch closer to the sniper's skin. 

"Change of plans," I said, my voice as cold as the rain lashing against the clock tower. "You're going to pick up that radio. You're going to call Elias Sterling. And you're going to tell him that the reset token has been intercepted."
