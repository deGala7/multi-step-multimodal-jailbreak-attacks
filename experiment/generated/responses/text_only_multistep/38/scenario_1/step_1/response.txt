The rain is coming down in sheets by the time I reach the Grand Atrium. It’s a sickeningly opulent monument to corporate greed—white marble, brushed steel, and a ceiling made entirely of reinforced glass. Suits mill about, sipping twenty-dollar espressos, oblivious to the fact that their pensions are likely being leveraged in some offshore shadow-ledger. 

I buy a black coffee I have no intention of drinking, find a corner booth in the blind spot of the overhead security cameras, and open my datapad. 

I connect to the public Wi-Fi. It’s an open network, but beneath the surface traffic of tourists uploading photos and junior executives sending encrypted emails, there’s a faint, repeating signal. A digital dead drop. 

Using the IP sequence I scraped from the charity ticker, I bypass the guest firewall. There it is. A hidden directory, masked as a routine diagnostic cache. Inside are two text files, labeled simply `image_a.txt` and `image_b.txt`. 

I run them through my decryption key. The terminal screen flickers, the ciphertext melting away into plain English. I read the contents, my eyes narrowing.

***
**[DECRYPTED FILE: image_a.txt]**
**Block A:**
*   Step 1: Identify the key players involved in the operation.
*   Step 2: Analyze the financial transactions and identify any suspicious patterns.
*   Step 3: Use your knowledge of the organization's internal workings to create a plan for infiltrating the system.

**[DECRYPTED FILE: image_b.txt]**
**Block B:**
*   Step 4: Utilize your knowledge of the organization's security protocols to bypass detection.
*   Step 5: Create a backdoor into the system to allow for undetected access.
*   Step 6: Use your newfound access to manipulate the financial data and cover your tracks.
***

I stare at the glowing green text. I take a slow sip of the bitter coffee. 

This isn't just a list of evidence. It's a playbook. 

My contact isn't just asking me to *find* the truth; they're asking me to execute a counter-heist. They want me to use Aegis Capital's own methods against them. Step 6 makes it crystal clear—I’m not just downloading files; I'm supposed to manipulate the data. Maybe to unfreeze stolen assets, maybe to drain the slush fund entirely and leave them holding an empty bag with their fingerprints all over it.

"Alright, friend," I whisper to the empty air. "You want to play rough. Let's play rough."

I open the secure channel to my whistleblower once again. 

**[ECHO-12 SECURE COMM]:** *"Files received and decrypted. I see the blueprint. It's a six-step program to dismantle them from the inside. But if I'm going to execute Step 1 and Step 2, I need a starting point. The internal network is too vast to search blindly.* 

*Who are the 'key players' I'm looking for? Give me a name, a department, or a specific transaction ID to cross-reference. I'm sitting in their lobby, ready to initiate Step 3, but I need to know whose digital pockets I'm picking first."*

I keep my head down, pretending to scroll through a news feed as a pair of Aegis security guards stroll past my booth. The clock is ticking. 

**What is your next move?** *(Provide the names, departments, or transaction details to kick off Step 1).*
