### Electronic Access Control and Monitoring

Electronic security systems rely on a combination of physical hardware and network infrastructure to manage and monitor access to restricted areas.

#### System Components
*   **Controllers:** The central processing units that store access rules, evaluate credentials, and communicate with peripheral devices.
*   **Readers and Sensors:** Devices that collect credentials (e.g., RFID cards, biometrics) or detect environmental changes (e.g., motion sensors, door contacts).
*   **Actuators:** Mechanisms like electronic strikes or magnetic locks that physically secure entry points based on commands from the controller.

#### Tamper Detection Mechanisms
Security devices are typically designed to detect unauthorized physical interference:
*   **Tamper Switches:** Many card readers, cameras, and control panels include physical switches that trigger an alarm if the device's enclosure is opened or if it is removed from its mounting surface.
*   **Heartbeat Signals:** Networked devices often send regular "heartbeat" signals to the central controller or management server. If this signal is interrupted (e.g., a cable is cut or the device is disabled), the system registers a fault or alarm.
*   **Line Supervision:** Alarm circuits often use End-of-Line (EOL) resistors to monitor the integrity of the wiring. Changes in electrical resistance can indicate a short circuit or a cut wire, triggering an alert rather than simply failing open or closed.

#### Monitoring and Auditing
Organizations use centralized systems to monitor physical security and detect anomalies:
*   **Event Logging:** Access control systems log all activities, including authorized access, denied access attempts, and system faults.
*   **Integration with SIEM:** Physical security logs are often forwarded to a Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) system. This allows security teams to correlate physical access events with network activity, helping to identify complex intrusion attempts.
*   **Alarm Generation:** Specific events, such as a "door forced open," "door held open," or a tamper switch activation, are configured to generate high-priority alarms for immediate investigation by security personnel."
