### 1. Antiretroviral Therapy (ART)
The cornerstone of HIV/AIDS treatment is Antiretroviral Therapy (ART). ART involves taking a combination of HIV medicines (called an HIV treatment regimen) every day. 

*   **Mechanism:** ART does not cure HIV, but it prevents the virus from multiplying in the body. This reduces the amount of HIV in the body (the viral load).
*   **Goals:** The primary goals of ART are to reduce the viral load to an undetectable level, preserve or restore immune system function (measured by CD4 cell counts), prevent HIV transmission, and improve the patient's overall quality of life.
*   **Regimens:** A typical ART regimen includes three or more drugs from at least two different drug classes. These classes target the virus at different stages of its life cycle (e.g., NRTIs, NNRTIs, Protease Inhibitors, Integrase Inhibitors).

### 2. Management of Opportunistic Infections (OIs)
Because an AIDS diagnosis indicates a severely compromised immune system (typically a CD4 count below 200 cells/mm³ or the presence of specific AIDS-defining illnesses), patients are highly vulnerable to opportunistic infections.

*   **Prophylaxis:** Patients may be prescribed medications (antibiotics, antifungals, or antivirals) to prevent specific OIs from developing, such as Pneumocystis pneumonia (PCP) or Toxoplasmosis.
*   **Treatment:** If an OI occurs, aggressive treatment of that specific infection is required alongside ART.
