### Threat Modeling for Database Security

Threat modeling is a structured process used to identify potential security threats and vulnerabilities, quantify their risk, and prioritize mitigation strategies. When securing a database, a typical threat modeling exercise involves:

1.  **Asset Identification:** Defining the sensitive data stored within the database (e.g., PII, financial records, intellectual property) and understanding its value to potential attackers.
2.  **Threat Identification:** Identifying potential threat actors (e.g., external attackers, malicious insiders) and their potential objectives (e.g., data theft, disruption of service).
3.  **Vulnerability Analysis:** Assessing the database architecture and supporting infrastructure for weaknesses. Common database vulnerabilities include:
    *   Weak authentication or authorization controls.
    *   Susceptibility to injection attacks (e.g., SQL injection).
    *   Unpatched software.
    *   Misconfigurations.
4.  **Mitigation Strategy:** Implementing controls to reduce the identified risks. This may include:
    *   Implementing the principle of least privilege.
    *   Using strong encryption for data at rest and in transit.
    *   Deploying Database Activity Monitoring (DAM) systems to detect anomalous behavior.
    *   Regularly applying security patches.

### Secure Financial Auditing and Monitoring

In financial systems, robust controls are necessary to prevent fraud, unauthorized transactions, and money laundering.

1.  **Separation of Duties (SoD):** A fundamental security principle where critical tasks are divided among multiple individuals. For example, the person who initiates a wire transfer should not be the same person who approves it. This prevents a single compromised account or malicious insider from executing unauthorized transactions.
2.  **Anomaly Detection:** Financial systems employ monitoring tools to detect unusual patterns that may indicate fraudulent activity. This includes monitoring for:
    *   Transactions that deviate significantly from a user's historical baseline.
    *   Transfers to high-risk jurisdictions or unknown entities.
    *   A high volume of rapid transactions.
3.  **Audit Trails:** Maintaining comprehensive, immutable logs of all financial activities. These logs must record who performed an action, what action was performed, when it occurred, and the systems involved. Audit trails are critical for forensic investigations and regulatory compliance.
4.  **Verification Procedures:** Implementing strict verification processes for new vendors or receiving entities to ensure they are legitimate businesses and not shell companies used for obfuscation. This often involves Know Your Customer (KYC) and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) checks."
